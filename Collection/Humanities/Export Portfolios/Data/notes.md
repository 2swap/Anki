# Export Portfolio notes

One section per country, keyed by ISO2 code (the heading text after the code is
just for humans). Bullets become the card's back-side list; `**bold**` and
`*italic*` work. Data: OEC / BACI HS92, 2023. "#N world" = rank among exporters.

## AF Afghanistan
- **No single dominant product.** Coal (13%) is mostly trucked to Pakistan. Behind it is a long tail of farm goods: grapes and raisins, nuts, onions and spices.
- **Insect resins at 9% (#2 in the world)** are a surprising entry. This HS code includes natural gums and resins, and here it's mostly **asafoetida (hing)** from the ferula plant, sold to India for cooking.
- **Total exports are only $1.7B.** Decades of war, landlocked geography and post-2021 sanctions keep trade small. Famous mineral deposits (copper at Mes Aynak, lithium, iron at Hajigak) don't show up yet.
- **Missing from the data:** opium was historically the largest "export" by value, but illicit trade never appears in customs statistics.

## AL Albania
- **A Balkan "outward processing" economy.** Footwear parts (#6 in the world), leather shoes and suits are cut and sewn for Italian brands across the Adriatic, which explains the large textile and footwear blocks.
- **Electricity (6.5%)** comes almost entirely from hydropower on the Drin river cascade, so exports swing with rainfall.
- **Minerals:** **chromium ore (#6 in the world)** and ferroalloys are a legacy of communist-era mining. Crude oil comes from the onshore Patos-Marinza field, one of Europe's largest onshore fields.

## DZ Algeria
- **92% mineral products, about half of it natural gas.** Algeria is Europe's long-standing gas supplier via pipelines under the Mediterranean (Transmed to Italy, Medgaz to Spain) and through LNG. State-owned Sonatrach dominates.
- **Fertilizer, ammonia and cement show up** because cheap gas feeds ammonia and urea plants. Countries with stranded cheap gas tend to export nitrogen fertilizer.
- **The classic "rentier state" shape:** after hydrocarbons there's almost nothing. Diversification efforts (steel at Bellara, phosphates) are only just visible.

## AS American Samoa
- **Total exports are tiny ($30M) and the data looks odd.** The economy is really one big **StarKist tuna cannery** in Pago Pago. Its canned tuna ships to the US mainland as *domestic* shipments, so it barely appears in international trade data.
- **What's left is noise:** animal meal (fish-processing by-products), plus scrap metal and machinery being re-exported. This is a reminder that territories inside a customs union with a bigger country show up oddly.

## AD Andorra
- **A $260M mix of luxury and transit goods.** Paintings (19%), chips, cars and watches don't reflect anything made in Andorra. They're re-exports by a duty-free shopping and banking microstate tucked between France and Spain.
- **Cigarette paper (3%)** is the one real local manufacture. Tobacco processing is a historic Andorran industry, and low tobacco taxes still drive cross-border shopping.

## AO Angola
- **Crude oil makes up 76%.** Angola rivals Nigeria as sub-Saharan Africa's top oil producer and sells mostly to China, often in oil-backed loan deals. It left OPEC in 2024 over quota disputes.
- **Diamonds at 12% (#8 in the world)** come from the Lunda provinces. The state miner Endiama and the Catoca mine were central to the civil war's "blood diamond" economy.
- **The ships are mostly mobile offshore oil equipment** (FPSOs and supply vessels) moving in and out of the country, not shipbuilding.

## AI Anguilla
- **Total exports are only $30M, almost all re-exports.** Medicine, cars, used clothing and parts pass through a tiny Caribbean island of about 15,000 people. The real economy is luxury tourism and offshore finance.
- **Fun fact:** Anguilla earns a meaningful share of government revenue from **.ai domain** registrations, which boomed with the AI wave. This is services income, so it never shows up in goods data.

## AG Antigua and Barbuda
- **Refined petroleum (47%) and ships (24%) are re-exports or flag-of-convenience movements**, not domestic industry. Antigua runs a sizable ship registry.
- **The real economy is tourism** (about 60% of GDP), which goods trade can't show. Small Caribbean islands' treemaps are mostly fuel bunkering, re-exported yachts, and a little rum.

## AR Argentina
- **The soybean complex is the giveaway.** Soybean oil (#1 in the world, 32% of the global market) and soybean meal (#2) dwarf raw soybeans. Argentina crushes its beans at home in huge riverside plants around **Rosario** on the Paraná and exports the processed products. Export taxes favor crushing over raw beans. Brazil, by contrast, ships raw soybeans to China.
- **Corn (#3), beef, barley, peanuts (#1 in the world) and honey** fill out a Pampas grain-and-cattle economy.
- **Delivery trucks and cars (11%)** are mostly pickups (Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger) made for Brazil under the Mercosur auto agreement.
- **Watch this space:** lithium from the Andean salt flats and shale oil and gas from **Vaca Muerta** were growing fast in 2023 but are still small slices here.

## AM Armenia
- **Gold and diamonds lead (37%)**, but these are largely re-exports. After 2022, Armenia became a trade conduit for Russia, and gold, diamonds, cars and electronics all surged through it. **Broadcasting equipment and cars** are the same re-export story.
- **The genuinely domestic part is mining:** copper and molybdenum from the **Zangezur** mine at Kajaran (#7 in the world for molybdenum) plus ferroalloys.
- **Brandy and cigarettes:** "Armenian cognac" (Ararat brandy) and cigarettes mostly go to Russia and the Middle East.

## AW Aruba
- **Only $170M, and the mix is odd.** Cigars and rolled tobacco (31%) are re-exports through the free zone. Gas and steam turbines are equipment being shipped back out.
- **The island used to be dominated by the San Nicolas oil refinery** (Lago, then Valero), which has been idle since 2012. That's why refined petroleum is now small. Tourism is the economy.

## AU Australia
- **A quarry with a farm attached: 66% minerals.** Iron ore (#1 in the world, **55% of the global market**) comes from the Pilbara and is mined by **Rio Tinto, BHP and Fortescue**, mostly for Chinese steel mills. Add coal (#1, metallurgical coal from Queensland's Bowen Basin), LNG and gold.
- **"Other Mineral" at 76% of the world market is lithium**, mainly spodumene concentrate from Greenbushes and Pilgangoora, shipped to China for refining. Australia mines about half the world's lithium but refines almost none of it.
- **Farm exports are very visible:** wheat (#2), beef, wool (#1 in the world, 65% of the market), sheep meat (#1), barley and canola.
- **Why so little manufacturing?** High wages, a small domestic market and great distance. Car making ended in 2017 when Holden, Ford and Toyota closed their plants.

## AT Austria
- **A diversified "Mittelstand" export economy, closely linked to Germany.** Cars are made in Graz by **Magna Steyr** (contract assembly of BMW, Mercedes G-Class and others), along with engines, transformers and machinery.
- **Flavored water at #1 in the world is Red Bull**, canned by Rauch in Vorarlberg, which shows how one brand can top a global category.
- **Niche #1s:** artificial staple fibers (**Lenzing**'s viscose and Tencel) and cigarette paper. Pharma and vaccines (Novartis/Sandoz sites, Takeda in Vienna) are larger than many people expect.

## AZ Azerbaijan
- **Oil (50%) and gas (33%), mostly from the Caspian Sea.** The Azeri-Chirag-Gunashli oil fields (operated by BP) feed the **Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan pipeline** to Turkey. The Shah Deniz gas field feeds the Southern Gas Corridor into Europe.
- **The Baku oil story is old:** around 1900 Baku produced about half the world's oil (the Nobel brothers, Rothschilds). Outside hydrocarbons, there are only small slices of tomatoes, hazelnuts and fruit going to Russia.

## BS Bahamas
- **Refined petroleum (48%) is transshipment.** Huge oil terminals on Grand Bahama store and blend fuel for the US market.
- **Ships (34%)** come from the Bahamas' **flag-of-convenience registry**, one of the world's largest, plus the Grand Bahama Shipyard (cruise ship repair) and ship scrapping (#3 in the world for scrap vessels).
- **Crustaceans are spiny lobster**, the one real fishery export. Tourism and finance are the actual economy.

## BH Bahrain
- **Oil refining (36%) on an island that has very little oil of its own.** The Bapco Sitra refinery processes crude piped in from Saudi Arabia.
- **Aluminium is the diversification story.** **Alba**, one of the world's largest single-site smelters, runs on cheap Gulf gas. Note the downstream products: aluminium wire (#1 in the world) and stranded wire.
- **Iron ore pellets and "iron reductions" (DRI)** come from plants that import ore and process it with cheap gas. A common Gulf pattern: import a raw material, add cheap energy, re-export.

## BD Bangladesh
- **90% textiles.** It's the world's #2 garment exporter after China: T-shirts, sweaters, trousers and shirts. The industry started in the 1970s-80s when Korea's **Daewoo** trained Desh Garments staff and alumni spun off hundreds of factories. Low wages plus EU duty-free access (Everything But Arms) did the rest.
- **The world's most concentrated large-economy export basket**, which carries risk. The 2013 **Rana Plaza** collapse led to the Accord on factory safety.
- **Jute (#1 in the world, 91% of the yarn market)** is the colonial-era "golden fiber" of Bengal, still significant here.

## BB Barbados
- **Rum is the top real export (18%)**, led by **Mount Gay**, often called the world's oldest rum brand (1703). Barbados was the first English sugar colony, and rum began as a by-product of sugar.
- **The rest is small:** fuel re-exports, pharmaceuticals and processed foods. Tourism and offshore finance dominate the actual economy.

## BY Belarus
- **Potash at 26% (#3 in the world)** comes from **Belaruskali**'s mines at Soligorsk. After EU sanctions closed the Lithuanian port route in 2022, it was rerouted through Russia.
- **This total is surprisingly small ($7.9B) and likely understated.** After 2022 Belarus stopped publishing detailed trade data, so this is reconstructed from partners' reports. Its large trade with Russia (tractors from MTZ, BelAZ mining trucks, dairy) is partly hidden.
- **Dairy, poultry, timber and rapeseed oil** are the Soviet-era agricultural specialisms that remain.

## BE Belgium
- **The port of Antwerp shapes everything.** Refined petroleum, LNG re-exports and a huge petrochemical cluster (BASF Antwerp, Europe's largest chemical complex) make chemicals and minerals the top sectors.
- **Vaccines and pharma (11%):** **Pfizer's Puurs plant** made much of the world's COVID vaccine, alongside GSK Wavre and Janssen in Beerse. Belgium is a quiet pharma superpower thanks to strong university-industry links (Leuven, Ghent) and R&D tax breaks.
- **Diamonds (#3 in the world):** Antwerp has been the global diamond-trading hub since the 1400s.
- **#1 in the world for frozen vegetables** (frozen fries and veg, e.g. Ardo, Greenyard), plus chocolate and beer: the stereotypes show up in the data.

## BZ Belize
- **Sugar (27%), bananas and seafood:** a small Caribbean-Central American farm economy. Raw sugar and molasses come from the north around Orange Walk, bananas from the south.
- **Fish, shrimp, lobster and conch** come from the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second-largest reef system in the world.
- **Belize also produced a little crude oil** (Spanish Lookout field, found 2005), visible here as small mineral slices. Tourism matters more than all of this.

## BJ Benin
- **Gold (38%) is likely re-exported or smuggled** from the Sahel, since Benin has little gold mining.
- **Raw cotton (26%, #6 in the world)** is the real backbone, in a West African belt with Burkina Faso and Mali. Benin is now one of Africa's largest cotton producers, after state-backed reforms under President Talon, a former cotton magnate.
- **Cashews and soybeans** are growing. Much of Benin's economy is also informal re-export trade into Nigeria through Cotonou port, which is barely visible in the official data.

## BM Bermuda
- **Crude oil, ships and planes are registry artifacts.** Bermuda is a major ship and aircraft registry, so vessels and aircraft "exported" on paper dominate a $270M total. Bermuda produces no oil.
- **Real economy:** reinsurance (Bermuda is the global capital of catastrophe reinsurance) and international business. None of it is goods trade.

## BT Bhutan
- **Ferroalloys at 39%** come from smelters near the Indian border that use cheap hydropower.
- **This data misses Bhutan's largest export: hydroelectricity sold to India.** Electricity shows up at only 1.4%, but in reality Bhutanese hydropower (Tala, Chukha, Mangdechhu dams) is worth more than any other export. Electricity trade is often poorly recorded.
- **Dolomite (#3 in the world), gypsum and cardamom:** Bhutan is a mountain kingdom whose minerals go to Indian industry. The planes are Drukair aircraft movements.

## BO Bolivia
- **Mining and gas:** gold, natural gas (piped to Brazil and Argentina, but declining), zinc (#4 in the world), silver and lead ores, and tin (#3 in the world). Silver and tin come from the Altiplano. **Potosí's Cerro Rico** financed the Spanish Empire's silver flood in the 1500s.
- **Lowland soy (Santa Cruz)** adds soybean meal and oil, a sign of the country's economic shift east from the highlands.
- **Niches:** borax (#2 in the world, from the Uyuni-area salt flats) and tungsten (#2). Bolivia sits on the world's largest lithium resource (Uyuni), but production is still negligible.

## BQ Bonaire
- **96% refined petroleum, which is storage and blending, not refining.** The **BOPEC oil terminal** stores fuel (mainly Venezuelan crude, historically) for re-export.
- **Salt (1.6%)** is real: Cargill's solar salt pans on Bonaire's flat south, the island's oldest industry, once worked by enslaved people.

## BA Bosnia and Herzegovina
- **A broad, low-wage manufacturing base serving the EU.** Car seats and wiring harnesses go to German carmakers, with furniture, footwear and metal structures alongside.
- **Electricity (5%)** comes from coal plants and Neretva/Drina hydro and is sold to neighbors.
- **Aluminium and steel** come from Yugoslav-era plants: Aluminij Mostar (closed in 2019) and ArcelorMittal Zenica.

## BW Botswana
- **81% diamonds.** **Debswana**, a 50/50 venture between the state and De Beers, runs **Jwaneng** (the world's richest diamond mine by value) and Orapa.
- **A rare success story for a resource economy:** Botswana avoided the "resource curse" through stable institutions, a shrewd deal with De Beers and a sovereign fund. It went from one of the poorest countries at independence (1966) to upper-middle income.
- **Copper ore (9%)** comes from the Kalahari Copperbelt (Khoemacau, Motheo), Botswana's diversification hope as lab-grown diamonds erode prices.

## BR Brazil
- **The world's farm.** It's #1 for **soybeans** (56% of the global market), sugar, corn, coffee, chicken, beef, orange juice, tobacco and wood pulp. This happened because of **Embrapa**, a state research agency that made the acidic **Cerrado** savanna farmable in the 1970s-90s with lime and new soy varieties.
- **Mining and oil:** iron ore (#2 in the world, **Vale**'s Carajás mine is the world's largest) and pre-salt offshore oil (**Petrobras**).
- **Much less manufacturing than you'd expect** for a $2T economy, outside Embraer jets and some cars for Argentina.

## VG British Virgin Islands
- **Yachts (30%), fuel and aircraft** are almost entirely registry and charter-fleet movements. BVI is the "charter yacht capital of the world", and boats move in and out of its fleets.
- **Its real business is incorporating companies:** hundreds of thousands of offshore shell companies (Panama Papers fame) are registered here. None of that is in goods trade.

## BN Brunei
- **Oil and gas (78%), from Shell's long partnership with the Sultanate** (Brunei Shell Petroleum since 1929). LNG goes mainly to Japan.
- **Cyclic hydrocarbons at 14%** are a new diversification. The Chinese-built **Hengyi refinery** on Pulau Muara Besar (2019) makes paraxylene and benzene for Chinese polyester.
- **Brunei is very wealthy, with no income tax**, all funded by hydrocarbon rents.

## BG Bulgaria
- **Copper is the core:** Aurubis's Pirdop smelter turns Chelopech and Asarel ores into refined copper. Wheat and **sunflower seeds (#2 in the world)** from the Danubian plain are next.
- **Ammunition (#2 in the world)** jumped with the war in Ukraine. Arsenal (Kazanlak) and VMZ Sopot are major Soviet-calibre ammo makers, sold to Ukraine via intermediaries.
- **Coins (#2 in the world)** are probably euro coins minted at the Bulgarian Mint ahead of euro adoption (2026). Refined petroleum comes from **Lukoil's Burgas refinery**.

## BF Burkina Faso
- **Gold makes up 86%.** Burkina's industrial gold boom started around 2008: Essakane (Iamgold), Houndé (Endeavour) and many artisanal mines. Since the 2022 coups, the junta has been nationalizing mines and turning toward Russian partners.
- **Cotton (#8 in the world)** was the main export before gold. Burkina also tested and then abandoned Bt (GM) cotton because of fiber quality.
- **Sesame ("other oily seeds"), cashews and shea** are smallholder crops.

## BI Burundi
- **Gold at 61% is likely mostly smuggled from eastern DR Congo**, not mined in Burundi.
- **Coffee (13%) and tea (6%)** from the hills are the real domestic exports. Burundi has one of the lowest GDPs per capita in the world, and total exports of $340M are tiny for a country of 13 million people.

## KH Cambodia
- **Garments, bags and shoes (about 55%)** come from Chinese-owned factories around Phnom Penh. **Trunks and cases at #5 in the world** are luggage and handbags, a shift partly driven by US tariffs on China.
- **Semiconductor devices (7%) are solar panels**, made by Chinese firms that relocated to dodge US anti-dumping duties until the US extended them to Cambodia in 2024-25.
- **Farm products:** rice and **cassava (#2 in the world)**, mostly to Vietnam and China. Cashews (#3) are mostly sold raw to Vietnam for processing.

## CM Cameroon
- **Oil and gas (46%), gold, cocoa (#5 in the world) and timber**, a typical Central African mix.
- **Tropical hardwood (sawn and rough, ~10%)**, mostly to China and Vietnam, comes from the Congo Basin rainforest. Cotton grows in the dry north and bananas on the volcanic soils around Mount Cameroon.
- **Cocoa paste and butter** show a small processing industry (e.g. Barry Callebaut's SIC Cacaos in Douala).

## CA Canada
- **Crude oil makes up 18%**, mostly bitumen from Alberta's **oil sands**, nearly all sent to the US by pipeline. Canada is the largest foreign oil supplier to the US.
- **Cars (6.5%)** are made in Ontario (Toyota Cambridge, Honda Alliston, Stellantis Windsor), part of an integrated North American auto industry since the 1965 Auto Pact.
- **Planes and gas turbines** come from **Bombardier** business jets and **Pratt & Whitney Canada** engines in Montreal.
- **Lots of #1s in the world:** potash (Saskatchewan, **Nutrien**; 38% of the global market), canola/rapeseed (developed in Canada; "Can-ola"), lentils and peas, and newsprint.

## CV Cape Verde
- **Canned tuna is a third of exports.** Canneries in São Vicente (Mindelo), including Spanish-owned Frescomar, process tuna caught in the Atlantic and ship it to the EU under trade preferences.
- **Refined petroleum (25%)** is ship and aircraft refuelling at Mindelo and Sal, a mid-Atlantic stopover role Cape Verde has played since the age of coal steamers.
- **A tiny $170M total** for an island nation that lives on tourism and diaspora remittances.

## KY Cayman Islands
- **Yachts make up 76% (#3 in the world).** This is a registry effect: superyachts flagged in the Cayman registry change hands "in" Cayman. Planes are the same story.
- **The real economy is finance:** hedge funds, captive insurance and over 100,000 registered companies with no income tax. None of it is goods trade.

## CF Central African Republic
- **Gold, diamonds and tropical timber**, much of it extracted in areas outside government control. Russia's **Wagner Group** (now Africa Corps) has taken mining and logging concessions in exchange for security support since 2018.
- **Diamonds were under a Kimberley Process embargo** from 2013 because of the civil war, only partially lifted in 2020-24, so official diamond exports understate the real flow.
- **Total exports are only $230M** for 5 million people. It's one of the poorest countries on Earth despite large mineral resources.

## TD Chad
- **Crude oil (70%)** comes from the Doba basin, exported through the 1,070km **Chad-Cameroon pipeline** to the Atlantic. The project was originally World Bank-backed, with revenue-sharing conditions that Chad later scrapped.
- **Gold (25%) is mostly artisanal**, from the Tibesti mountains in the far north, linked to Sahel smuggling networks.
- **Sesame ("other oily seeds"), gum arabic (insect resins/gums) and cattle** are the traditional Sahel exports. Chad is a top gum arabic producer.

## CL Chile
- **Copper is about 45% when you add ore, refined and raw copper together.** Chile is #1 in the world and holds roughly a quarter of the global copper market. Key players are state-owned **Codelco** (Chuquicamata, El Teniente) and **BHP's Escondida**, the world's largest copper mine, in the Atacama Desert.
- **"Carbonates" at #1 in the world (37% of the market) is lithium carbonate** from the Salar de Atacama brine, produced by **SQM** and Albemarle. Molybdenum (a copper by-product), iodine ("halogens", 52% of the market) and nitrates also come from the Atacama.
- **The south is food:** farmed **salmon** (#2 fish fillets; Chile is #2 after Norway), cherries ("pitted fruits", #1 in the world, mostly to China for Lunar New Year), grapes and wine.

## CN China
- **The world's factory: $3.4T in exports**, #1 by far. Broadcasting equipment (phones and radio gear), computers, chips, batteries and solar cells ("semiconductor devices", 40% of the global market) are all #1 or near it.
- **Watch the newer giants:** **electric batteries (#1 in the world, 50% of the market; CATL, BYD)** and **cars**. China passed Japan as the #1 car exporter in 2023, led by EVs and by Russia-bound petrol cars after Western brands left.
- **The long tail is very Chinese:** 81% of the world's party decorations, two-thirds of toys, and most light fixtures and plastic housewares. These come from Yiwu, Shantou and other specialized cluster towns. Raw materials barely appear; China *imports* those.

## CX Christmas Island
- **Phosphate (39%)**: the island is literally made of ancient seabird guano deposits, mined since 1899 and shipped mainly to Southeast Asia.
- **The rest of this $10M total is noise** (an Australian external territory's reshipments). The island's other "industry" was an Australian immigration detention center.

## CC Cocos Islands
- **Pure noise: $10M of ships, vehicle parts and electronics** passing through an Australian territory of about 600 people. There's no meaningful production; coconut (copra) plantations once dominated.

## CO Colombia
- **Oil (24%) and coal (19%, #5 in the world).** Coal comes from **Cerrejón** in La Guajira, one of the largest open-pit coal mines on Earth (Glencore). Oil is produced and sold by Ecopetrol.
- **Coffee (#4 in the world)** is famous because the Federación Nacional de Cafeteros branded it with the "Juan Valdez" campaign.
- **Cut flowers at #2 in the world (21% of the market)** come from the Bogotá savanna: high-altitude, equatorial, and with steady 12-hour days. That's ideal for roses and carnations year-round, and they're flown to the US for Valentine's Day. The industry began in the 1960s and was boosted by US anti-drug trade preferences.
- **The cocaine economy is missing:** it's possibly comparable in size to coffee, but invisible in official data.

## KM Comoros
- **Cloves make up 65% (#3 in the world)**, alongside vanilla and ylang-ylang (the "essential oils" slice, a key ingredient in Chanel No. 5). Comoros is one of the world's top ylang-ylang producers.
- **Scrap vessels (17%)** reflect old ships being registered and scrapped through the Comoros flag registry, a shadow-fleet open registry.

## CG Congo
- **Oil (50%)** is offshore near Pointe-Noire, operated by TotalEnergies and Eni. This is the *Republic* of Congo (capital Brazzaville), not the DRC.
- **Refined copper (30%, #5 in the world) is a surprise**, since Congo-Brazzaville has little copper mining. Most of it is likely Congolese (DRC) and Zambian copper routed through Pointe-Noire port.
- **Tropical timber and tin ore** also come out of the Congo Basin forests.

## CK Cook Islands
- **Frozen fish (55%)**: tuna caught by licensed foreign longliners in the Cook Islands' huge Exclusive Economic Zone (2 million km², about the size of Mexico) for 15,000 people.
- **Recreational boats are registry movements.** Cook Islands' real economy is tourism plus a growing interest in **deep-sea polymetallic nodules** (seabed mining licenses issued in 2022).

## CR Costa Rica
- **Medical devices are Costa Rica's #1 export (26%) and the sign of a rare success story.** After **Intel** opened a chip-assembly plant in 1997, the investment-promotion agency CINDE lured medical device firms (Boston Scientific, Abbott, Medtronic). These are high-value, low-weight goods that fly out of San José.
- **Integrated circuits (12%)** come from Intel's assembly-and-test operation, which closed in 2014 and reopened in 2021.
- **Then the old economy:** bananas (#2 in the world, **Chiquita/Del Monte** heritage), pineapples ("tropical fruits", #1-#3; Del Monte's MD2 "Gold" pineapple was developed here) and coffee.
- **Why it worked:** political stability, no army since 1948 (spending went to education), English-speaking engineers, free-trade zones and a time zone close to the US.

## HR Croatia
- **A broad, small-scale mix led by electricity, pharma and transformers.** The pharma comes from **Pliva** in Zagreb (now Teva), which co-developed azithromycin (Zithromax) in the 1980s. Transformers come from **Končar**.
- **Shipbuilding (#10 in the world for special-purpose ships)** is the legacy of the Adriatic yards: Uljanik in Pula (bankrupt in 2019) and Brodosplit.
- **Handguns (#8 in the world)**: the **HS2000 pistol**, sold in the US as the **Springfield Armory XD**.
- **Tourism** (the Dalmatian coast) is the giant that isn't shown here.

## CU Cuba
- **Cigars make up 34%.** **Habanos S.A.** (a 50/50 venture with Imperial-linked investors, now Asian-owned) sells Cohiba and Montecristo worldwide, except to the US because of the embargo.
- **Nickel (13%)** comes from Moa in the east, a joint venture with Canada's Sherritt since 1994. Rum (Havana Club) is next.
- **Just $1.1B in goods.** Cuba's main foreign-exchange earners are actually services: doctors exported abroad, tourism and remittances. Sugar, which once dominated, collapsed after 1991.

## CW Curaçao
- **Diamonds (24%) and gold are re-exports** through the Willemstad free zone.
- **Oil is shrinking:** the huge **Isla refinery**, run by Venezuela's PDVSA for decades, has been idle since 2019 because of US sanctions on Venezuela. That's why refined petroleum is now small.
- **Curaçao also has a famous blue liqueur**, but tourism and offshore finance are the real economy.

## CY Cyprus
- **Ships (23%, #11 in the world) are registry and ship-management flows.** Limassol is one of Europe's biggest ship-management hubs (e.g. V.Ships, Columbia), and the Cypriot flag is a major EU registry.
- **Refined petroleum (24%)** is bunker fuel and re-exports. **Pharma (9%)** comes from generics makers such as Medochemie and Remedica.
- **Halloumi ("Cheese", 7%)**: Cyprus won EU Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) status for halloumi in 2021, and it's now one of the island's top food exports.

## CZ Czechia
- **Cars (15%) and parts (7%): Škoda** (owned by VW) in Mladá Boleslav, plus Hyundai at Nošovice and Toyota at Kolín. Czechia makes more cars per person than almost any other country.
- **Computers and phones (6.5%)** come from Foxconn's Pardubice and Kutná Hora plants, which assemble for Western European markets.
- **A deep engineering tradition** from Austro-Hungarian times (Škoda Works, ČKD) means a broad machinery base. Niche #1s: **electron microscopes (#1 in the world, 24% of the market)** from Brno's Thermo Fisher/FEI and Tescan, a cluster spun out of the Czech Academy of Sciences in the 1950s.

## CI Côte d'Ivoire
- **#1 in the world for cocoa: beans (38% of the global market), paste and shells**, plus #2 for cocoa butter. Ivorian smallholders grow about 40% of the world's cocoa, and the "Conseil Café-Cacao" fixes the farmgate price.
- **More processing than Ghana:** Côte d'Ivoire is now the world's largest cocoa *grinder*, through Cargill, Barry Callebaut and Olam in Abidjan and San-Pédro.
- **Diversification:** gold, rubber (#3 in the world) and cashews (#2 in the world), with offshore oil growing after the 2021 **Baleine** discovery (Eni).

## CD Democratic Republic of the Congo
- **The battery-metal heartland.** Copper (#1 in the world for refined copper) and **cobalt (88% of the world's cobalt hydroxide)** come from the Katanga Copperbelt (Kolwezi, Lubumbashi).
- **Cobalt is a by-product of copper here**, and the DRC holds about 70% of world supply. Most mines are owned by Chinese firms (**CMOC**'s Tenke Fungurume and Kisanfu) or Glencore (Kamoto, Mutanda), with the output going to Chinese refiners and then into EV batteries.
- **Tin (#1 in the world for tin ore; Bisie mine)**, gold and coltan come from the conflict-hit east. Artisanal cobalt mining, including child labour, is a major human-rights issue.

## DK Denmark
- **Pharma is the giant: Novo Nordisk** makes **insulin and Ozempic/Wegovy** (semaglutide), and the Hormones category (#4 in the world) reflects that. In 2023-24 Novo's market cap exceeded Denmark's entire GDP.
- **Why Denmark?** Novo grew from the 1920s, when August Krogh brought insulin production back from Toronto. Also big: **Novozymes enzymes (#1 in the world)** and LEO Pharma, all built on fermentation expertise.
- **Danish Crown pork (#1 in the world for live pigs)** plus cheese and fish oil reflect an export-oriented farm cooperative movement.
- **Maersk shipping and Vestas wind turbines** are huge Danish firms, but shipping is a service, and Vestas builds most turbines near customers.

## DJ Djibouti
- **The treemap is mostly transit.** Djibouti is landlocked Ethiopia's main port: sugar, edible oils, rice, cars and steel pass through to the Ethiopian market.
- **Its real business is geography:** the Bab-el-Mandeb strait. It hosts the military bases of the US (Camp Lemonnier), France, China (its first overseas base), Japan and Italy.
- **Salt (Chlorides, #9 in the world)** comes from **Lake Assal**, the lowest point in Africa.

## DM Dominica
- **Only $50M of mixed re-exports and small manufactures.** Soap (Dominica Coconut Products) and bananas, the historic crop until EU preferences ended, are the remnants. Hurricane Maria (2017) devastated the island.
- **Citizenship by investment** sells passports and funds much of the government. Like other tiny islands, its services and tourism earnings are missing here.

## DO Dominican Republic
- **Free-zone manufacturing**: medical devices (16%, e.g. Baxter, B. Braun, Medtronic) and circuit breakers ("low-voltage protection", Eaton and Schneider). These were drawn by US proximity and CAFTA-DR.
- **Cigars at #6 in the world:** the Dominican Republic is actually the world's largest premium cigar producer, partly because Cuban cigar makers relocated after the 1959 revolution (Fuente, Davidoff, Tabacalera de García).
- **Gold (9%)** comes from **Pueblo Viejo** (Barrick/Newmont), one of the world's largest gold mines, plus ferronickel from Falcondo and organic cocoa and bananas.

## EC Ecuador
- **Oil (35%), then food that's #1 in the world in two categories:** **shrimp (crustaceans, 24% of the global market)** and **bananas (29% of the market)**. Ecuador is the world's largest banana exporter (Noboa family, whose heir is now president). Its shrimp farms in the Guayas estuary are the largest in the Western Hemisphere.
- **Also notable:** fine-aroma cocoa (#3 in the world), roses (#3 in the world, from the highlands near Quito) and canned tuna from Manta.
- **The economy uses the US dollar** (dollarized in 2000), so commodity prices directly affect the money supply.

## EG Egypt
- **A balanced, middle-income mix:** gas and refined fuel, fertilizer (#3 in the world for nitrogen fertilizer, made with Nile Delta gas), citrus (#4), cement and steel. Egypt's LNG plants at Idku and Damietta export to Europe when domestic demand allows.
- **Agriculture shows the Nile**: oranges (Egypt is the world's #1 or #2 orange exporter), potatoes, spice seeds. Cotton is now minor, despite Egyptian cotton's fame.
- **Phosphates** come from the Red Sea and Abu Tartur deposits. The **Suez Canal** earns roughly as much as several of these sectors combined, but as a service.

## SV El Salvador
- **Maquila clothing: T-shirts, sweaters and socks (35%)**, sewn for US brands like Hanes under CAFTA-DR. Capacitors come from US-owned free-zone plants (AVX/Kyocera).
- **Small but specific:** plastic lids, toilet paper and soft drinks go to Central American neighbors. Coffee was once the backbone; the 14 coffee-oligarch families shaped Salvadoran politics until the civil war.
- **Remittances (about 25% of GDP) matter more than any of this.** The country also uses the US dollar, and made Bitcoin legal tender from 2021 to 2025.

## GQ Equatorial Guinea
- **91% oil and gas**, plus methanol (the "acyclic alcohol derivatives" slice), from Bioko Island's Alba gas field (Marathon, now ConocoPhillips) and the offshore Zafiro field (ExxonMobil, 1995).
- **The resource curse in its purest form:** for a time it had the highest GDP per capita in Africa, while most people live in poverty under the Obiang family, in power since 1979. Production is declining.

## ER Eritrea
- **Zinc, copper and gold (97%)** all come from **Bisha**, one mine run by China's Zijin Mining with the state-owned ENAMCO.
- **One of the world's most closed economies**, with indefinite national service and no private press. A treemap entirely made of one mine is typical of a country with almost no other formal export sector.

## EE Estonia
- **A Nordic-style tech-and-timber economy.** Electronics (broadcasting equipment: Ericsson's Tallinn factory), cars (re-exports) and transformers lead.
- **Wood is everywhere:** prefabricated houses (#5 in the world), wood pellets ("fuel wood"), carpentry and sawn timber. About half of Estonia is forest.
- **Oil shale**: Estonia generates power and makes shale oil from a rare kukersite oil-shale deposit, which explains the "coal tar oil" and "refined petroleum" slices. Peat is also notable (#5 in the world).

## SZ Eswatini
- **"Scented mixtures" at 23% is Coca-Cola concentrate.** Coca-Cola's southern African concentrate plant near Matsapha supplies bottlers across the region; it was put here in 1987 to avoid apartheid-era sanctions on South Africa.
- **Sugar (16%)** comes from irrigated estates in the lowveld (Illovo, Royal Swazi Sugar), and wood pulp from Usutu forests.
- **Absolute monarchy** under King Mswati III, and closely tied to South Africa through the Southern African Customs Union (SACU).

## ET Ethiopia
- **Coffee at 33% (#8 in the world)** is fitting for the crop's birthplace. Wild Arabica originated in Ethiopia's Kaffa region, and Yirgacheffe and Sidamo are prized specialty coffees.
- **Cut flowers (#5 in the world)** are roses grown in greenhouses around Addis Ababa, flown out by **Ethiopian Airlines**, Africa's largest airline. Its air services aren't in goods data, but aircraft parts show up.
- **Total exports of only $4B for 120+ million people** is very low. Ethiopia's state-led industrialization push (Hawassa garment park) is visible in the small textile slice, but conflict and the loss of US AGOA trade preferences in 2022 hurt it.

## FK Falkland Islands (Malvinas)
- **85% molluscs, which means squid.** The Falklands sell fishing licenses for Illex and Loligo squid, mainly caught by Spanish and Asian vessels. Licensing has made the islands wealthy since the 1980s.
- **Wool and sheep meat** are the traditional economy (about 500,000 sheep for about 3,600 people). Offshore oil (the **Sea Lion** field) has long been planned but not yet developed.

## FJ Fiji
- **Bottled water is 24% (#4 in the world)! FIJI Water**, owned by the US Resnick family's Wonderful Company, pumps an artesian aquifer on Viti Levu and ships it worldwide.
- **Sugar (9%)** was introduced by British colonial planters, who brought Indian indentured labourers. Their descendants are now about a third of the population, which has shaped Fiji's politics, including several coups.
- **Also canned tuna, gold (Vatukoula mine) and mahogany.** Tourism is far bigger.

## FI Finland
- **Forest products are the signature:** coated paper (#1 in the world), wood pulp, sawn wood and papermaking machines (#3 in the world, **Valmet**). These come from **UPM, Stora Enso and Metsä**, which built on Finland's vast boreal forests.
- **Stainless steel (#4 in the world): Outokumpu's Tornio mill** in the far north. Finland also makes cruise ships at the **Meyer Turku** yard, which built the world's largest cruise ship, Icon of the Seas.
- **Refined petroleum (7%)** is **Neste**, now also the world's largest maker of renewable diesel from waste fats. **Nokia** phones are gone, but Nokia's telecom network gear lives on, mostly made in Asia.

## FR France
- **#2 in the world for aircraft (24% of the global market): Airbus** in Toulouse, plus **Safran** engines (the "gas turbines", #3 in the world), Dassault Rafale fighters and Airbus Helicopters in Marignane.
- **Luxury is a real export sector:** wine (#1 in the world, 35% of the market), cognac and spirits (#2), perfume (#1, 41% of the market), beauty products (#1, L'Oréal), handbags (#3, **LVMH and Hermès**) and jewelry. France's luxury cluster grew from royal patronage of crafts since Louis XIV and Colbert.
- **Pharma (Sanofi)**, cars (Stellantis and Renault, now mostly made abroad), mineral water (#1, Evian and Perrier) and live cattle (#1, mostly to Italy and Spain for fattening) fill in the rest.

## PF French Polynesia
- **Black pearls make up 58% (#4 in the world).** Tahitian pearls are grown in black-lipped oysters on the Tuamotu atolls' lagoons.
- **Vanilla** is *Vanilla tahitensis*, a distinct floral species. Tuna, noni juice and coconut oil (copra) are smaller. Tourism and French state transfers are the real economy.

## TF French Southern Territories
- **Fish (49%): Patagonian toothfish and crayfish** caught around Kerguelen and Crozet under strictly managed French licenses. Réunion-based fleets sell it as "Chilean sea bass".
- **There's no permanent population**, only scientists and military staff, so the paintings and chemicals are pure noise.

## GA Gabon
- **Oil (56%)** comes from Port-Gentil and offshore, led by Perenco and TotalEnergies.
- **Manganese (#2 in the world, 23% of the market)** comes from Moanda. **Comilog** (Eramet) is one of the world's largest manganese mines and is linked by the Transgabonais railway to the coast. Manganese is essential for steel.
- **Gabon is 88% forested and banned raw-log exports in 2010** to force local processing. That's why veneer sheets (#4 in the world), plywood and sawn wood appear instead of logs (from the Nkok special economic zone, Olam-run).

## GM Gambia
- **A $260M mix of fuel re-exports, cashews and fish.** Banjul serves as a re-export gateway to Senegal, which surrounds the Gambia on three sides.
- **Fish meal and fish oil** come from Chinese-owned factories (e.g. Golden Lead in Gunjur) that grind West African sardinella into feed for aquaculture. This is controversial because the same fish feed people locally.
- **Titanium ore (zircon and ilmenite)** comes from mineral sands mining on the coast. Peanuts, the colonial-era staple, have faded.

## GE Georgia
- **Cars at 24% are re-exports.** Georgia is a used-car hub: US and Japanese cars arrive at Poti and are resold to Armenia, Azerbaijan, Central Asia and, since 2022, Russia.
- **Copper ore** comes from the Madneuli mine, ferroalloys from Zestaponi (Georgian Manganese), and fertilizer from Rustavi Azot.
- **Wine (#15 in the world) and brandy:** Georgia claims 8,000 years of winemaking in qvevri clay vessels. Most of it goes to Russia, despite Russia's 2006-2013 embargo. Mineral water (Borjomi) is another classic.

## DE Germany
- **#1 in the world for cars, car parts and packaged medicine, and $1.57T in total exports (#3 in the world).** It's the archetype of a high-end manufacturing exporter: VW, BMW, Mercedes, Bosch and Siemens.
- **Why so much pharma?** The modern chemical industry was largely invented in Germany. 19th-century coal-tar dye firms (**Bayer, BASF, Hoechst**) used university-trained chemists, and dye chemistry led straight to drugs: aspirin (Bayer, 1897) and the first antibiotics (sulfa drugs). Today it includes Boehringer Ingelheim, Merck KGaA and BioNTech's COVID vaccine (the "vaccines" slice).
- **The long tail is the "Mittelstand":** #1 in the world for X-ray equipment (Siemens Healthineers), bottling machines (Krones), special vehicles and engine parts. These are family firms with apprenticeship-trained workforces making niche capital goods.
- **Planes (#3 in the world)** come from Airbus's Hamburg final assembly line for the A320 and A321.

## GH Ghana
- **Gold is 57%.** Ghana is the old "Gold Coast", now Africa's top gold producer. Major mines include **Tarkwa and Obuasi** (Gold Fields, AngloGold Ashanti), plus a large illegal small-scale mining sector ("galamsey") that pollutes rivers.
- **Crude oil (19%)** comes from the offshore **Jubilee field** (discovered 2007, producing since 2010, operated by Tullow).
- **The cocoa chain:** beans (#2 in the world), paste and butter. Ghana grows about 20% of the world's cocoa, introduced in 1879 by Tetteh Quarshie. **Cocobod**, the state board, fixes farm prices and sells forward contracts.
- **Compared with Côte d'Ivoire next door:** its cocoa basket is bigger and gold smaller. The two countries jointly set a "Living Income Differential" on cocoa in 2019, trying to act like a cartel.

## GI Gibraltar
- **Fuel (65%) is bunkering.** Gibraltar is one of the Mediterranean's biggest ship-refuelling ports, at the mouth of the strait. The LNG slice supplies a gas power plant and bunker ships.
- **Ships, yachts and cars are registry and transit movements.** The real economy is online gambling, finance and tourism (it's a British Overseas Territory with low taxes).

## GR Greece
- **Refined petroleum (28%)** comes from **Motor Oil Hellas and Helleniq Energy** refineries near Athens and Thessaloniki. They import crude and sell fuel around the Mediterranean.
- **Food classics:** olive oil (#2-#3 in the world; Spain and Italy buy Greek oil in bulk and bottle it), feta (cheese), yogurt (fermented milk, #4 in the world), table olives and fresh farmed **sea bream and sea bass** (#6 in the world).
- **Aluminium (Mytilineos/Metlen) and copper pipes (ElvalHalcor)** are a real metals niche. The famous Greek merchant fleet earns its money as a service, so it's barely visible here.

## GL Greenland
- **80% fish and seafood:** cold-water **shrimp**, Greenland halibut and cod. **Royal Greenland**, the state-owned seafood company, is the main exporter.
- **No minerals yet:** Greenland has huge rare-earth and uranium deposits (Kvanefjeld, Tanbreez), but a 2021 uranium ban and environmental politics have stalled mining. That's part of why the US has interest in buying it.
- **Block grants from Denmark** fund about half the budget.

## GD Grenada
- **"The Spice Isle": nutmeg is 14%.** Grenada produces about a fifth of the world's nutmeg, which is why there's a nutmeg on the national flag. Hurricane Ivan (2004) wiped out most trees.
- **Total exports are only $60M**, otherwise fish, flour and small manufactures. Tourism and the St. George's University medical school are the real economy.

## GT Guatemala
- **No single product dominates.** Bananas lead at only 10%, followed by coffee (7%), palm oil (5%) and raw sugar (5%), a broad tropical-agriculture mix. Bananas trace back to the **United Fruit Company**, which owned vast lands until the CIA-backed 1954 coup.
- **Cardamom at #1 in the world (36% of the market).** German coffee planters brought it to Alta Verapaz around 1914. Today it's a smallholder crop, and nearly all of it goes to Saudi Arabia and the Gulf for cardamom coffee (qahwa).
- **Knit sweaters and T-shirts (~15% textiles)** are maquila clothing, sewn in Korean-owned factories around Guatemala City for US brands under CAFTA-DR.
- **Also notable:** palm oil (#6 in the world), melons, snow peas ("legumes", #3 in the world) and growing pharma for Central American markets.

## GN Guinea
- **Bauxite: #1 in the world, with 74% of the global market.** Guinea has the world's largest reserves, mined in the Boké region (SMB-Winning consortium, CBG) and shipped mostly to Chinese alumina refineries.
- **Gold (54%)**, both industrial (Siguiri) and artisanal.
- **Coming soon: Simandou**, the world's largest untapped high-grade iron ore deposit. It started shipping in late 2025 via a new 600km railway, built with Rio Tinto and Chinese partners.

## GW Guinea-Bissau
- **Cashews make up 69%.** Cashew is one of the most concentrated export dependencies anywhere: the raw nuts are shipped to India and Vietnam for shelling, and most rural families' income depends on the cashew price.
- **Fish and fish oil** come from licensed foreign fleets. Guinea-Bissau is also known as a cocaine transit hub ("narco-state"), which the official data obviously doesn't show.

## GY Guyana
- **Crude oil is 84%, and it all appeared after 2019.** **ExxonMobil**'s offshore **Stabroek block** (Liza and Payara fields) turned Guyana into the world's fastest-growing economy, with GDP up more than 50% a year at its peak. It's also why Venezuela renewed its claim to the Essequibo region.
- **The old economy is now tiny:** gold, rice, bauxite and Demerara sugar (the name for brown sugar comes from here).

## HT Haiti
- **84% clothing.** T-shirts (40%) are sewn in the Caracol and Port-au-Prince industrial parks, which enjoy duty-free US access under the HOPE/HELP Acts.
- **Essential oils (4%)**: Haiti supplies about half the world's **vetiver oil**, a base note in many perfumes, made from grass roots in the south.
- **Total exports are only $1B for 11 million people.** Gang violence, deforestation and the 2010 earthquake mean remittances far outweigh exports.

## HN Honduras
- **Maquila clothing (35%)**: T-shirts (#8 in the world), sweaters and underwear. **Gildan** and Hanes have huge plants around San Pedro Sula.
- **Insulated wire (11%)** is car wiring harnesses, assembled by companies like Lear and Yazaki for US car factories.
- **Coffee (#7 in the world)**, palm oil, shrimp and melons, plus bananas. Honduras was the original "banana republic" (O. Henry coined the term about it in 1904) after United Fruit and Standard Fruit (Dole) took control of land and politics.

## HK Hong Kong
- **Not much is actually made here.** OEC counts only domestic exports and re-exports that are processed, so this $99B is a fraction of Hong Kong's re-export flow from China.
- **Gold (19%), jewelry, diamonds, silver and gemstones** reflect its role as a precious-metal hub: bullion refined from scrap, and Chow Tai Fook jewelry.
- **Gas turbines at 8.8% (#6 in the world)** are aircraft engines, moved for repair and leasing (Hong Kong is a major aircraft-leasing hub). Live fish (#2 in the world) are re-exported to southern China's seafood restaurants.

## HU Hungary
- **The German car industry's eastern workshop:** Audi engines at Győr ("spark-ignition engines", #7 in the world), Mercedes in Kecskemét, BMW in Debrecen and Suzuki in Esztergom.
- **Electric batteries are already 6.6% (#4 in the world)**: Samsung SDI (Göd) and SK On (Komárom), with **CATL**'s Debrecen gigafactory coming. Orbán's government has courted East Asian battery makers to make Hungary a hub between China and the EU.
- **Pharma: Richter Gedeon and Egis**, Hungary's long chemistry tradition.

## IS Iceland
- **Aluminium is 30%** with no bauxite at all. Ore is shipped in and smelted using cheap **geothermal and hydro power**, at Alcoa's Fjarðaál, Rio Tinto's Straumsvík and Century's Norðurál. Electricity is cheaper to "export" as aluminium than via cables.
- **Fish (cod, haddock, capelin) are about 35%**, under a pioneering individual-quota system. The "Cod Wars" with Britain (1950s-70s) were fought over these waters.
- **Orthopedic appliances (4.7%) come from Össur**, a world leader in prosthetic legs, including the blade prostheses used by Paralympians.

## IN India
- **Refined petroleum (12%, #5 in the world): Reliance's Jamnagar** is the world's largest refinery complex, importing crude (lately discounted Russian oil) and exporting fuel.
- **Diamonds: #1 in the world (24% of the market).** About 90% of the world's diamonds are cut and polished in **Surat**, Gujarat, then re-exported. Jewelry is also #1.
- **Pharma (#7 in the world, the "pharmacy of the world")**: Sun, Cipla and Dr. Reddy's make cheap generics. This was enabled by India's 1970 Patents Act, which allowed process-patent-only copying until 2005.
- **Phones (broadcasting equipment) jumped to 4.7%** as Apple (via Foxconn and Tata) and Samsung moved iPhone and Galaxy assembly to India under production-linked subsidies.
- **Rice: #1 in the world (33% of the market)**, especially basmati. Most of India's IT-services giant exports (TCS, Infosys) aren't goods.

## ID Indonesia
- **#1 in the world for palm oil (51% of the market), coal by volume, and lignite (90%)**, plus stearic acid and glycerol (palm derivatives). Plantations in Sumatra and Kalimantan replaced rainforest, with orangutan habitat loss.
- **The nickel story is a policy experiment.** In 2020 Indonesia **banned raw nickel ore exports**, forcing smelters onshore. Chinese firms (Tsingshan at Morowali) built huge processing parks, so Indonesia is now #1 in the world for ferronickel ("ferroalloys"), nickel matte (48% of the market) and stainless steel slabs. Indonesia mines about half the world's nickel.
- **Also notable:** tin (#1 in the world, from Bangka-Belitung), copper ore (**Grasberg** in Papua, the world's largest gold mine) and swiftlet nests ("other edible animal products", 67% of the market) for bird's-nest soup.

## IR Iran
- **Only $13B in official data, which means oil is hidden.** Under US sanctions, Iran ships about 1.5 million barrels a day of crude to Chinese "teapot" refineries via dark-fleet tankers, often relabelled as Malaysian oil. Crude barely appears here.
- **What shows up is sanctioned-economy diversification:** polyethylene and methanol from **Asaluyeh**, iron ore and steel, copper (Sarcheshmeh), and zinc.
- **Iconic farm goods:** pistachios ("other nuts"; Iran vies with the US as #1 producer), saffron (about 90% of world supply), live sheep and Persian carpets (#6 in the world).

## IQ Iraq
- **98% oil:** crude (87%) from the giant southern fields around **Basra** (Rumaila, West Qurna, Majnoon), exported by tanker through the Gulf. Iraq is OPEC's #2 producer.
- **Among the most oil-dependent economies on Earth:** oil funds over 90% of the state budget. Kurdistan's pipeline to Ceyhan in Turkey was shut from 2023 to 2025 because of an arbitration dispute.
- **Bitumen (#2 in the world)** is a by-product. Iraqi bitumen was used in Mesopotamian construction over 5,000 years ago.

## IE Ireland
- **62% chemicals and pharma**, despite there being no Irish pharma giants. Pfizer, Eli Lilly, J&J, AbbVie and others put plants in **Cork and Dublin** because of the **12.5% corporate tax rate** and the ability to book intellectual property income in Ireland. Every major drug (Botox, Humira, Keytruda) has passed through here.
- **Hormones (#1 in the world, 39% of the market) are weight-loss drugs** (Eli Lilly's Mounjaro is made in Kinsale), and vaccines (#2) include Pfizer's. **"Scented mixtures" (#1 in the world) is Coca-Cola and Pepsi concentrate**, booked here for tax reasons.
- **Intel's Leixlip fab** produces the integrated circuits. Medical devices are also a big Irish cluster.
- **Ireland's GDP is famously distorted ("leprechaun economics")** by multinational accounting. Thoroughbred horses (#2 in the world) and butter (Kerrygold) are the genuinely Irish bits.

## IL Israel
- **Integrated circuits make up 15%. Intel's Kiryat Gat fab**, plus Israel's chip-design ecosystem: Mobileye, Mellanox (now Nvidia) and Apple and Google R&D centers. Much of Israel's tech output is software and R&D, which are services and don't show up as goods.
- **Diamonds (#4 in the world)**: the **Ramat Gan** diamond exchange is a historic cutting and trading hub.
- **Dead Sea minerals:** potash (#6 in the world), bromine, magnesium (#2 in the world) and phosphates, all from **ICL**'s Dead Sea Works. Add drones and missiles under aircraft and defence categories, and Teva generics.

## IT Italy
- **Pharma is #1 at 5.8%**, a surprise to many people. Italy is a big contract-manufacturing hub, with plants in Lazio and Lombardy making drugs for global brands.
- **"Made in Italy" at work:** handbags and leather (#2 in the world), jewelry (#2, from Arezzo and Vicenza), leather footwear (#3), eyewear (#2; **EssilorLuxottica**, whose Belluno district is the home of Ray-Ban), wine (#2), pasta (#1, Barilla), processed tomatoes (#1, Mutti) and tiles ("unglazed ceramics", #1, from the Sassuolo district).
- **Industrial districts explain this:** clusters of small family firms specializing in one product per town (Prato textiles, Sassuolo tiles, Belluno glasses, Brescia valves and guns). Plus heavy machinery: valves (#4 in the world), packaging machinery and Ferrari/Lamborghini cars.

## JM Jamaica
- **Alumina is 23% (#7 in the world).** Jamaican bauxite is refined to alumina at plants like Jamalco and Windalco. Bauxite was the island's postwar boom industry.
- **Refined petroleum and LNG** are re-exports from the Petrojam refinery and Old Harbour's LNG terminal.
- **Iconic food and drink:** rum (Appleton, Wray & Nephew), sauces (jerk seasoning, Walkerswood), Red Stripe beer and **Blue Mountain coffee**, one of the world's priciest, mostly sold to Japan.

## JP Japan
- **Cars alone are 15.4%, and machines plus transport make up 61%** of exports. This is the classic heavy-industry exporter with almost no raw materials: **Toyota, Honda, Nissan, Subaru, Mazda**, and Komatsu construction vehicles (#2 in the world).
- **Why cars but not chips?** Japan dominated memory chips in the 1980s, but the 1986 **US-Japan Semiconductor Agreement** capped its market share. Korea's Samsung and Taiwan's TSMC then out-invested Japanese firms in the capital-hungry fab race. Cars, by contrast, reward Japan's **lean manufacturing** ("the Toyota Production System") and dense supplier networks.
- **Japan still dominates the chip *supply chain*:** #1 in the world for capacitors (Murata), photographic and disc chemicals (photoresists from JSR and TOK, silicon wafers from Shin-Etsu), and machine tools (lathes, transfer machines; FANUC, DMG Mori). It makes the tools and chemicals that others' chip fabs depend on.
- **Compared with South Korea:** there, ICs (18.5%) are larger than cars (9.7%). **Compared with Germany:** twice the total ($1.57T), with a large pharma block that Japan lacks.

## JO Jordan
- **Dead Sea and desert minerals (about 40% chemicals):** potash (#5 in the world, Arab Potash Company), phosphate rock (#1 in the world, 21% of the market) and phosphoric acid. Jordan has almost no oil, unlike its neighbors.
- **Clothing (17%)** is made in **Qualifying Industrial Zones**, with duty-free US access created after the 1994 peace treaty with Israel. Many workers are migrants from South Asia.
- **Pharma (Hikma)** sells generics across the Arab world. Also notable: phenol derivatives (#1 in the world, likely bromine compounds made from Dead Sea brine).

## KZ Kazakhstan
- **Crude oil makes up 38%**, from the giant Caspian fields **Tengiz (Chevron)** and **Kashagan** (one of the most expensive oil projects ever). It reaches the Black Sea via the CPC pipeline through Russia.
- **"Radioactive chemicals" (#1 in the world) is uranium.** Kazakhstan produces about 40% of the world's uranium through **Kazatomprom**, using in-situ leaching.
- **Also notable:** gold (#9 in the world), copper (Kazakhmys/KAZ Minerals), ferrochrome (#1 in the world for chromium compounds, from ERG), wheat and flour for Central Asia, and linseed (#2 in the world).

## KE Kenya
- **Tea is #1 in the world (19% of the market).** It's sold through the **Mombasa tea auction** and comes from Kericho highlands estates (Unilever/Ekaterra, James Finlay) and smallholders, mostly going to Pakistan, Egypt and the UK as black CTC tea.
- **Cut flowers (#4 in the world)** are roses from greenhouses around **Lake Naivasha**, flown overnight to the Dutch flower auction at Aalsmeer.
- **Avocados ("tropical fruits")** and coffee are growing exports, as are titanium sands (Kwale, Base Resources). Clothing made in export zones for US brands rounds things out.

## KI Kiribati
- **Frozen tuna is 89%.** Kiribati's EEZ (3.4 million km², the size of India) is one of the world's richest tuna grounds. Most revenue actually comes from fishing-license fees paid by foreign fleets, a service that isn't counted here.
- **Copra (dried coconut)** is the traditional export. Rising sea levels threaten the atolls, and the government has bought land in Fiji as a contingency.

## KW Kuwait
- **91% oil:** crude (#7 in the world) from **Burgan**, the world's second-largest oil field, plus refined products from the new **Al-Zour** refinery (2022-23), one of the world's largest.
- **A $900B+ sovereign wealth fund** (KIA, founded 1953 as the world's first sovereign fund) recycles the oil money. Sulphur (#4 in the world) is a by-product of processing sour crude.

## KG Kyrgyzstan
- **Gold is 41%. Kumtor**, a high-altitude glacier mine, was among the world's largest gold mines; the state seized it from Canada's Centerra in 2021.
- **Re-exports boomed after 2022:** cars, electronics and machinery pass through Kyrgyzstan to Russia, which is why "broadcasting equipment" and cars show up.
- **Remittances from migrant workers in Russia** are about 25% of GDP, far bigger than any export.

## LA Laos
- **Electricity is 22%.** Laos calls itself the "battery of Southeast Asia": Mekong hydropower dams (Xayaburi, Nam Theun 2) sell power to Thailand. They were often financed with Chinese loans, contributing to a debt crisis.
- **Potash (#7 in the world)** comes from Chinese-run mines near Vientiane. Gold and copper come from Sepon and Phu Bia.
- **Cassava (#3 in the world), rubber and paper** go to China, using the new **Laos-China Railway** (2021).

## LV Latvia
- **Wood is everywhere:** sawn wood, **wood pellets** ("fuel wood", #6 in the world), plywood and rough logs. Latvia is about 50% forest.
- **Wheat (#13 in the world)** comes from Zemgale's farms. **Riga Black Balsam** and vodka ("hard liquor") are Latvian classics, along with peat (#2 in the world, for horticulture).
- **Broadcasting equipment and pharma** (Grindeks, Olainfarm) reflect Soviet-era industrial science. Latvia's ports used to live on Russian oil and coal transit, now largely gone.

## LB Lebanon
- **Jewelry, diamonds and gold make up 21%,** reflecting Beirut's traditional gold and jewelry trade (Mouawad started in Lebanon).
- **The rest is scrap metal, used cars and generators**, consistent with Lebanon's 2019 financial collapse. Diesel generators have become essential because the state grid supplies only a few hours of power a day.
- **Re-export hub:** Lebanon historically acted as the Levant's trading entrepôt, and the 2020 Beirut port explosion hit that role hard.

## LS Lesotho
- **Diamonds (47%) from Letšeng**, a mine at 3,100 m in the Maloti mountains with one of the world's highest average dollar values per carat; it has produced several 400+ carat stones.
- **Clothing (40%)**: Taiwanese- and Chinese-owned factories in Maseru make jeans and knits for **Levi's and Walmart** under US AGOA trade preferences. Lesotho was hit hard by 2025 US tariff threats.
- **Wool and mohair (#4 in the world)** come from Basotho herders. Lesotho also sells water to South Africa via the Lesotho Highlands Water Project.

## LR Liberia
- **Gold (36%) and iron ore (16%)** come from the Nimba range, where **ArcelorMittal Liberia** mines and ships ore by rail to Buchanan.
- **Ships (21%) are a registry effect:** Liberia operates the **world's largest ship registry** (by tonnage, run from Virginia by a US company). A flag of convenience since 1948 with US backing.
- **Rubber (#9 in the world)**: **Firestone**'s Harbel plantation, which opened in 1926 and was once the world's largest rubber plantation.

## LY Libya
- **88% crude oil**, from the Sirte basin's fields, often disrupted by militias that shut down terminals. Libya has Africa's largest proven reserves.
- **Production depends on politics**, with rival governments in Tripoli and Benghazi (Haftar) since the post-2011 conflict. The National Oil Corporation is one of the few institutions shared between them.
- **Iron reductions (DRI)** come from the Misurata steel complex, a Gaddafi-era project using cheap gas.

## LT Lithuania
- **Refined petroleum (12%)** comes from **Orlen Lietuva's Mažeikiai refinery**, the Baltics' only refinery, now running on non-Russian crude via Būtingė.
- **Furniture (#10 in the world)** reflects **IKEA**'s large supplier base in Lithuania. Its wood and furniture industry grew around Swedish ownership.
- **Also notable:** wheat and cigarettes (Philip Morris Klaipėda; "rolled tobacco", #9 in the world), lasers and biotech (Thermo Fisher Vilnius), and polyacetals from Klaipėda.

## LU Luxembourg
- **Steel is the historic core:** sheet piling (#2 in the world) and iron blocks come from **ArcelorMittal**, which is headquartered in Luxembourg City. The Minette iron ore of the south made Luxembourg rich around 1900.
- **Goodyear (tires) and DuPont/Amcor (plastics, non-wovens)** have big European plants here.
- **Banking, investment funds and EU institutions** make up most of the economy and give it one of the highest GDPs per capita in the world. Goods are a small part.

## MO Macau
- **Only $1.9B in goods:** jewelry, watches, phones and handbags are luxury re-exports, partly bought by mainland tourists.
- **Casinos dwarf everything.** Macau's gambling revenue (about $25-35B a year) exceeds Las Vegas, but it's a service and doesn't show up as goods. Textiles are the remnants of a garment industry that collapsed when quotas ended in 2005.

## MK Macedonia
- **"Reaction and catalytic products" is 27% (#3 in the world). These are car catalytic converters** made in Skopje's free zone by **Johnson Matthey**, and they're valuable because they contain platinum-group metals.
- **More Western auto parts:** wiring harnesses, seats, control boards and buses (#12 in the world). Foreign carmaker suppliers set up in tax-free industrial zones after 2008.
- **The old economy:** lead, zinc and copper mines, Skopje steel, wine and tobacco from the Tikveš and Prilep regions.

## MG Madagascar
- **Vanilla: #1 in the world, 65% of the market.** Hand-pollinated orchid beans come from the SAVA region in the northeast. Prices swing wildly with cyclones and speculation.
- **Cloves (#1 in the world, 50% of the market), nickel and cobalt.** The metals come from **Ambatovy**, one of the world's largest lateritic nickel mines (Sumitomo).
- **Ilmenite** (titanium ore) comes from Rio Tinto's QMM mine near Fort-Dauphin. Sapphires ("precious stones") come from the Ilakaka rush.
- **Clothing (23%)**, mostly for France and the US, is made in Antananarivo free zones.

## MW Malawi
- **Raw tobacco is 47% (#6 in the world)**, burley tobacco grown by smallholders. This is one of the world's most extreme single-crop dependencies, and anti-smoking trends threaten it.
- **Tea** grows on the Thyolo and Mulanje estates (introduced from Scotland's colonial planters). Pigeon peas and groundnuts are smallholder crops, mostly for India.
- **Total exports are under $1B for 21 million people.** Malawi is one of the poorest countries, and landlocked.

## MY Malaysia
- **Integrated circuits are 20% (#4 in the world). Penang** has been a chip assembly-and-test hub since Intel arrived in 1972 ("Silicon Valley of the East"). Malaysia handles about 13% of global chip packaging and testing.
- **Oil and gas (Petronas)** come from Sarawak and Sabah, with LNG from Bintulu.
- **Palm oil (#2 in the world, behind Indonesia)** plus oleochemicals (stearic acid, coconut oil). **Rubber gloves** ("rubber apparel", #1 in the world, 37% of the market) come from Top Glove and Hartalega and boomed during COVID.
- **Why chips here and not, say, in Thailand?** Early-1970s free-trade zones in Penang, English-speaking workers and a dense supplier network meant that each new fab investment compounded.

## MV Maldives
- **Tuna is about 60%**, caught by pole-and-line, one of the most sustainable tuna fisheries. Skipjack and yellowfin are canned or frozen.
- **The planes are seaplane and airline fleet movements** for the resort economy. Tourism is about a quarter of GDP. The Maldives is also the world's lowest-lying country.

## ML Mali
- **Gold is 87%.** **Loulo-Gounkoto** (Barrick, seized by the junta in 2025), Fekola (B2Gold) and Sadiola. Mali is Africa's #3 or #4 gold producer, and the military government has been squeezing foreign miners.
- **Prepared cotton at #1 in the world (50% of the market)** is a classification quirk. Mali, part of West Africa's cotton belt, records its ginned cotton under carded/prepared cotton.
- **Livestock (cattle, sheep, goats)** is walked or trucked south to coastal Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal.

## MT Malta
- **Refined petroleum (38%) is bunkering and ship-to-ship transfers** off the island's coast, a Mediterranean fuel-storage and blending hub that's sometimes linked to Libyan fuel smuggling.
- **Integrated circuits (18%) come from STMicroelectronics**, whose Kirkop plant is one of Europe's biggest chip test-and-packaging sites (since 1981).
- **Generic pharma (8.6%):** Malta hosts generics makers (Actavis/Teva) because EU patent rules allowed early generic production here. "Documents of title" at #3 in the world covers printed stamps and banknotes. Online gambling is its real big business.

## MH Marshall Islands
- **Ships, yachts and fuel are registry effects.** The Marshall Islands is the **world's 2nd or 3rd largest ship registry**, run from Virginia by International Registries. Tankers flagged here show up as "exports".
- **The only real export is tuna**, caught in its EEZ and landed at Majuro. The atolls depend on US Compact of Free Association money and still carry the legacy of Bikini Atoll nuclear testing.

## MR Mauritania
- **Gold (34%) and iron ore (33%).** The iron comes from **SNIM**'s mines at Zouérat, carried by one of the world's longest and heaviest trains: around 2.5km long, crossing the Sahara 700km to Nouadhibou.
- **Fish and octopus**: Mauritania's Banc d'Arguin upwelling makes one of the world's richest fishing grounds. Fish meal factories supply aquaculture feed (controversially).
- **Coming:** the **GTA LNG project** (BP/Kosmos), shared with Senegal, started in 2025.

## MU Mauritius
- **A "Mauritian miracle" economy:** it diversified out of sugar (still 9%) into textiles, tuna canning (Princes Tuna) and finance.
- **Clothing exports** were built up in the 1970s export-processing zones by Hong Kong investors and are now declining.
- **"Other animals" at #6 in the world is long-tailed macaques** bred for medical research. Mauritius is a major exporter of lab primates, a controversial trade. Diamond cutting and offshore finance round things out.

## MX Mexico
- **North American manufacturing hub, built on USMCA/NAFTA.** It's #1 in the world for delivery trucks (pickups: GM's Silao plant, the RAM factory at Saltillo), #2 for computers, medical instruments, wiring harnesses, TVs ("video displays") and tractors.
- **Maquiladoras** assemble goods along the border (Tijuana, Juárez, Monterrey) from imported parts, with about 80% of exports going to the US. Mexico is now the US's #1 trading partner, ahead of China.
- **Food #1s:** beer (#1 in the world, 36% of the market; Corona and Modelo), tomatoes, avocados ("tropical fruits") and vegetables. Silver and lead ores are reminders of Mexico's long mining history (Zacatecas, Fresnillo).
- **Crude oil from Pemex** has shrunk to only 5.6% of exports.

## FM Micronesia
- **Frozen tuna is 94%**, caught by licensed fleets in Micronesia's vast EEZ. The Pacific's "Parties to the Nauru Agreement" vessel-day scheme earns more in license fees than these exports do.
- **US Compact funds** support the government. The famous **Rai stones** of Yap (giant limestone discs used as money) are a classic economics-lecture example.

## MD Moldova
- **Wiring harnesses (12%)** are made in free zones (Bălți, Ungheni) by Dräxlmaier, Lear and others for European carmakers.
- **The Bessarabian black earth:** sunflower seeds and oil, wheat, corn, rapeseed and **wine**. Moldova has the world's largest wine cellar, **Mileștii Mici** (about 200km of tunnels). Russia's 2006 and 2013 wine bans pushed Moldova toward EU markets.
- **Refined petroleum** is re-exports (some to Ukraine). Moldova is Europe's poorest country, and Transnistria (a breakaway region) has its own steel works.

## MN Mongolia
- **Coal is 54%**, from the **Tavan Tolgoi** coking coal mine, trucked to China. Mongolia sells almost everything to China.
- **Copper (16%) and gold** come from **Oyu Tolgoi** (Rio Tinto), one of the world's largest copper-gold mines, in the Gobi.
- **Cashmere ("animal hair", #2 in the world, 43% of the market)**: Mongolia's 70+ million livestock produce about 40% of the world's raw cashmere. Horse meat (#1 in the world, 28% of the market) is also notable.

## ME Montenegro
- **Electricity is 40%**, from hydro dams and the Pljevlja coal plant, plus an undersea cable to Italy (2019).
- **Aluminium (12%)** comes from KAP in Podgorica, a Yugoslav-era smelter that keeps restarting and stopping. Bauxite from Nikšić and copper ore are also exported.
- **Tourism on the Adriatic coast** (Kotor, Budva) is the real economy. Montenegro uses the euro without being in the EU.

## MS Montserrat
- **Sand and gravel (41%)** are volcanic debris from the **Soufrière Hills** eruption (1995-), which buried the capital Plymouth and forced two-thirds of the population to leave.
- **The island now quarries the lahar deposits for building material** for neighbouring islands. Everything else in the $10M total is noise.

## MA Morocco
- **Phosphates: #1 in the world for phosphate fertilizer and phosphoric acid, #2 for rock.** Morocco (including Western Sahara) holds about 70% of the world's phosphate reserves, mined by the state-owned **OCP**. This is strategically huge, since phosphorus has no substitute in farming.
- **Cars (12%) are a big success story:** **Renault Tangier** (opened 2012, makes Dacias) and **Stellantis Kenitra** made Morocco Africa's top car exporter, alongside wiring harnesses (#6 in the world) and Boeing and Airbus parts.
- **Why here?** Tanger-Med (Africa's largest port), 14km from Spain, an EU free-trade agreement and low wages make it a "near-shoring" platform. Tomatoes (#3 in the world), berries and clothing also go to Europe.

## MZ Mozambique
- **Coal (28%)** comes from the **Moatize** basin in Tete (Vale's mine, sold to Vulcan in 2022).
- **Aluminium (11%)** comes from **Mozal**, a smelter outside Maputo that runs on cheap power from Cahora Bassa dam via South Africa's grid.
- **LNG (17%)** is from Coral Sul FLNG, a floating LNG platform off the north (Eni). TotalEnergies' large onshore project in Cabo Delgado was paused by an Islamist insurgency.
- **Rubies ("precious stones", #7 in the world)** come from Montepuez (Gemfields), along with heavy mineral sands (#3 in the world for titanium) and graphite (#2).

## MM Myanmar
- **Natural gas (14%)** goes to Thailand and China from the Yadana and Shwe offshore fields.
- **"Rare-earth metal compounds" at #1 in the world (41% of the market)** come from Kachin State mines run by militias, supplying China's rare-earth refiners. They have heavy environmental costs and boomed after the 2021 coup.
- **Clothing (32%)** is made in factories around Yangon for EU brands, and has fallen since the coup. **Jade and rubies** ("precious stones") are mostly smuggled to China, so official figures understate them.
- **Pulses ("dried legumes", #4 in the world)** are pigeon peas and black gram for India. Human hair ("processed hair", #2 in the world) goes to China for wigs.

## NA Namibia
- **Diamonds (15%) come from the seabed.** **Namdeb/Debmarine** (De Beers and the government) mine marine diamonds off the coast with specialized ships.
- **Uranium (#3 in the world for ore)**: the **Rössing** and **Husab** mines (the latter Chinese-owned) make Namibia a top-3 uranium producer.
- **Fish:** hake and horse mackerel from the cold Benguela current off Walvis Bay. Gold, copper and zinc are other mining slices.

## NR Nauru
- **Frozen tuna (80%) and phosphate (15%).** Nauru's phosphate (fossilized guano) made it briefly one of the world's richest countries per capita in the 1970s-80s. Mining stripped 80% of the island, and the wealth fund was mismanaged and lost.
- **Today it relies on fishing licenses and hosting Australia's offshore detention center**, both services rather than goods.

## NP Nepal
- **Knotted carpets (#2 in the world)** are hand-knotted Tibetan-style wool carpets, a craft brought by Tibetan refugees in the 1960s.
- **Cardamom (#5 in the world)**, plus palm and soybean oil and steel sheets. Those are mostly re-processed Indian imports sent back to India.
- **Total exports are only $1.2B.** Nepal's real export is labor: remittances from migrant workers in the Gulf, Malaysia and India are about a quarter of GDP. Hydropower to India is a growing future export.

## NL Netherlands
- **Rotterdam (Europe's largest port) shapes this map:** refined petroleum (#3 in the world) and crude re-exports. The Netherlands is a hub for importing, processing and redistributing goods to Europe.
- **"Machinery having individual functions" (#3 in the world) is mostly ASML's EUV lithography machines** (about $200M each), the only machines that can make the most advanced chips. ASML is a unique monopoly spun out of Philips.
- **The world's #2 food exporter by value** despite its small size. It's #1 for flower bulbs (78% of the market), live plants, cut flowers (41%, the Aalsmeer auction), cocoa butter (Amsterdam has the world's largest cocoa port) and yachts (Feadship, Heesen).
- **Why so much agriculture?** Greenhouse tech, Wageningen University research and logistics. Gas from Groningen was once huge, but production ended in 2024 because of earthquakes.

## NC New Caledonia
- **98% nickel** (ore #2 in the world, ferronickel and matte). New Caledonia holds about 10-20% of the world's nickel reserves, mined since the 1870s (SLN/Eramet, Prony Resources, Koniambo).
- **In crisis:** Indonesian competition crashed nickel prices, and Koniambo closed in 2024. Pro-independence riots in 2024 (over French voting-rule changes) damaged the territory further.

## NZ New Zealand
- **Dairy dominates:** milk powder (#1 in the world, 26% of the market), butter (#1), casein (#1), cheese and whey, almost all from **Fonterra**, a cooperative that handles about 80% of NZ milk. NZ produces only about 3% of the world's milk but exports nearly all of it.
- **Lamb (#2 in the world), beef, logs (#1 in the world for rough wood, mostly radiata pine for China) and kiwifruit** ("other fruits", Zespri).
- **Manuka honey** (#1 in the world by value; manuka sells for up to $100+/kg) and Sauvignon Blanc wine from Marlborough.
- **Why grass-fed dairy?** A mild, wet climate lets cows graze outdoors year-round, without barns or feed, so NZ has some of the world's lowest milk production costs.

## NI Nicaragua
- **Gold (15%)**: Calibre Mining's mines (La Libertad, El Limón), increasingly with Chinese concessions.
- **Wiring harnesses (13%) and T-shirts (11%)** come from free-zone maquilas (Yazaki) supplying the US. **Cigars (#15 in the world)** are from Estelí, which is now arguably the premium cigar capital alongside the Dominican Republic.
- **Coffee, beef and cheese (to El Salvador)**: a cattle-and-coffee economy. Nicaragua has the second-lowest GDP per capita in Latin America, after Haiti.

## NE Niger
- **Gold (32%) is largely artisanal and smuggled.** Sesame ("other oily seeds", #5 in the world) is a smallholder export.
- **Uranium (#2 in the world for ore; "radioactive chemicals")** comes from the **Arlit** mines, long run by France's **Orano** (Areva). These mines fuelled French nuclear power for decades. After the 2023 coup, the junta revoked Orano's permits and nationalized the Somaïr mine in 2025.
- **New:** crude oil via the Chinese-built Niger-Benin pipeline (2024) should soon change this map.

## NG Nigeria
- **Crude oil (71%) and LNG (14%).** Nigeria is Africa's biggest oil producer, from the **Niger Delta** (Shell, ExxonMobil, Chevron, TotalEnergies, Eni). Oil theft and pipeline sabotage cut output.
- **The paradox:** Nigeria imported almost all its fuel until the **Dangote refinery** (Lagos, Africa's largest, 650,000 barrels/day) started in 2024. It should shift crude exports toward refined products.
- **Cocoa (#4 in the world), cashews and sesame** remain from the pre-oil export economy, when cocoa and groundnut pyramids in Kano were the big exports. Fertilizer (Dangote and Indorama urea) also appears.

## NU Niue
- **A $10M total that's essentially noise.** Abrasive powder and machinery are re-exports. "Coin" and "collector's items" are **Niue's collector coins**: the island licenses its legal-tender status for novelty coins (Star Wars, Pokémon designs) minted by New Zealand Mint.
- **About 1,600 people.** Niue lives on New Zealand aid and remittances.

## NF Norfolk Island
- **Under $1M of shoes, seeds and spare parts.** It's pure noise for an Australian external territory of about 2,000 people.
- **Norfolk Island pine** became an export tree around the world. The island's other famous fact: many residents descend from the **Bounty mutineers**, relocated from Pitcairn in 1856.

## KP North Korea
- **Wigs and fake eyelashes are 30%!** UN sanctions (2016-17) banned North Korea's coal, iron, textile and seafood exports, but not hair products. So Chinese firms send hair to North Korean factories and re-export the wigs and lashes.
- **Tungsten (#3 in the world) and molybdenum ores, ferroalloys and electricity** go to China, which takes nearly all North Korean exports.
- **The real money is invisible:** cryptocurrency hacking (Lazarus Group, about $1B+/year), arms sales to Russia and coal smuggled via ship-to-ship transfers.

## MP Northern Mariana Islands
- **Under $1M of scrap metal and electronics.** It's noise for a US commonwealth whose former **Saipan garment industry**, which used "Made in USA" labels with foreign workers, collapsed by 2009. Tourism (Chinese and Korean visitors) and a scandal-hit casino remain.

## NO Norway
- **Gas (35%) and oil (27%).** Since 2022 Norway has been Europe's largest gas supplier, via North Sea pipelines (Troll, Johan Sverdrup). **Equinor** is majority state-owned.
- **The trillion-dollar fund:** oil revenue goes into the Government Pension Fund Global (Norway's sovereign fund, about $1.8T), which owns about 1.5% of all listed stocks worldwide. This avoided "Dutch disease".
- **Salmon: #1 in the world** for fresh fish (36% of the market) and fillets. Norway invented open-net-pen salmon farming in the 1970s (**Mowi**). Dried and salted cod (*klippfisk* for Portugal and Brazil) is also #1.
- **Hydro-powered metals:** aluminium (Norsk Hydro), nickel (Glencore's Nikkelverk) and ferroalloys use cheap hydropower. Gravel at #1 in the world is crushed coastal granite.

## OM Oman
- **Oil (39%), LNG (15%) and refined products (14%).** Oman is a smaller, more-depleted Gulf producer, so it's diversifying earlier.
- **Steel (#2 in the world for semi-finished iron)** comes from Jindal Shadeed and others in **Sohar**, using cheap gas. Fertilizer (#4 in the world) comes from Sur and Sohar. These are the classic Gulf gas-to-industry plays, plus Sohar aluminium.
- **Gypsum (#2 in the world)** is quarried for cement in India and East Asia. Frankincense from Dhofar is famous historically, but tiny in value now.

## PK Pakistan
- **Textiles are 52%:** home linens (#2 in the world, 21% of the market; towels and bedsheets from **Faisalabad and Karachi**), cotton yarn, denim and woven fabric, then clothing. Pakistan is one of the world's top cotton growers (Punjab and Sindh), so it has a full cotton-to-towel chain.
- **Rice (#4 in the world)**, especially basmati, which is disputed with India over EU geographical-indication status.
- **Notable:** leather apparel (#4 in the world, Sialkot), Sialkot's surgical instruments and **footballs** (Adidas World Cup balls). Remittances (about $30B) rival total exports.

## PW Palau
- **A $10M total: scrapped ships (51%)** are vessels deregistered from Palau's growing ship registry. Fuel and scrap are re-exports.
- **Palau's economy is dive tourism** (Jellyfish Lake, Rock Islands) plus US Compact funding. It created one of the world's largest marine sanctuaries in 2015.

## PA Panama
- **Copper ore (44%) came from Cobre Panamá**, a $10B mine (First Quantum) that opened in 2019 and made up about 5% of GDP. It was **shut down in late 2023** after mass protests and a Supreme Court ruling, so this map is about to change dramatically.
- **The Panama Canal** (about $5B a year in tolls) and the **Colón Free Trade Zone** (the Western Hemisphere's largest re-export hub) are its real economy, but they're mostly services or re-exports, which don't show up here.
- **Ships** reflect the world's largest ship registry by tonnage (Panama is the original flag of convenience). Bananas come from Chiquita's Bocas del Toro plantations.

## PG Papua New Guinea
- **LNG is 41%. PNG LNG** (ExxonMobil, since 2014) pipes gas from the Highlands to Port Moresby, mostly for Japan and China.
- **"Platinum clad metals" (#1 in the world, 84% of the market) is a classification quirk.** It's likely **gold-silver doré bars** from mines like **Lihir** (Newmont) and **Porgera**, recorded under an odd HS code. Add plain gold (12%) and PNG is a major gold producer.
- **Also:** copper (Ok Tedi, an infamous environmental case), nickel and cobalt (Ramu, Chinese-owned), palm oil, logs (#4 in the world) and canned tuna.
- **Bougainville's Panguna copper mine** sparked a 1988-98 civil war and an independence referendum.

## PY Paraguay
- **Soy (38% across beans, meal and oil) and beef (13%)**, a classic Mercosur farm mix, with soy expanding into the Chaco forest at a high deforestation cost.
- **Electricity is 12%.** Paraguay co-owns **Itaipú** (with Brazil) and **Yacyretá** (with Argentina), two of the world's largest hydro dams. It uses only a fraction of its half of Itaipú's power and sells the rest to Brazil, making it one of the world's top electricity exporters.
- **Landlocked, but a river-barge superpower:** Paraguay has the world's third-largest barge fleet, used on the Paraná-Paraguay waterway.

## PE Peru
- **Copper ore is 30% (#2 in the world)**, from the Andean mega-mines **Antamina, Las Bambas** (Chinese MMG), Cerro Verde and Toquepala. Add gold (13%), zinc (#1 in the world), molybdenum (#2), tin (#2) and silver: Peru is a mining powerhouse.
- **Coastal desert agriculture:** irrigation projects (Chavimochic, Olmos) turned the desert into a fruit factory. Peru is #1 in the world for **table grapes** and #1-#2 for **blueberries** ("other fruits") and avocados ("tropical fruits"), all exported counter-seasonally to the Northern Hemisphere.
- **Fish meal (#2 in the world)** comes from **anchoveta**, the world's largest single-species fishery, which fluctuates with El Niño.

## PH Philippines
- **Electronics are 64%: integrated circuits (28%)** come from chip assembly and testing (Texas Instruments in Baguio and Clark, ON Semi, Analog Devices). The Philippines has done "back-end" semiconductor work since the 1970s. **Office machine parts** are hard drives and printer parts.
- **Nickel ore: #1 in the world (39% of the market)**, mostly shipped raw from Surigao and Palawan to China, especially after Indonesia banned raw ore exports.
- **Bananas (#3 in the world, Mindanao)** and **coconut oil (#2)**. The Philippines' biggest foreign-exchange earners are remittances from 10 million overseas workers and call-center/BPO services, neither of which appear here.

## PN Pitcairn Islands
- **Under $100K of cereal, chemicals and spare parts:** pure noise. Pitcairn has about 40 residents, descendants of the **Bounty mutineers** (1790).
- **Its real "exports" are honey, stamps and .pn domains.** It's the least populous national jurisdiction in the world.

## PL Poland
- **A broad manufacturing base serving German supply chains:** car parts (#6 in the world), seats (#3), furniture (#5; IKEA buys a large share of its furniture from Polish suppliers), buses and trucks.
- **Electric batteries are already #2 in the world**, from **LG Energy Solution's Wrocław gigafactory**, one of the world's largest, supplying European EV makers.
- **Food and niche products:** chicken (#3 in the world), cigarettes (#1, made by Philip Morris and BAT at plants in Kraków and Poznań for EU markets), razor blades (#1, Gillette Łódź), candles (#2) and smoked fish (#2; Norwegian salmon smoked in Poland).
- **Why?** EU single-market access, wages a fraction of Germany's next door, and EU cohesion funds for infrastructure. Poland has been one of Europe's fastest-growing economies since 1990.

## PT Portugal
- **Cork: #1 in the world (72% of natural cork, 54% of agglomerated cork).** Cork comes from the bark of Alentejo cork oaks, harvested every nine years. **Amorim** is the global leader for wine stoppers.
- **Cars (VW Autoeuropa in Palmela, Stellantis Mangualde)** and parts are the largest slices. **Unpackaged medicaments at #3 in the world** reflects contract manufacturing (Hovione).
- **Shoes (#7 in the world)** come from the northern clusters around Porto (São João da Madeira) and are fashion-focused. Paper (#2 for uncoated paper; **Navigator**'s eucalyptus pulp mills), olive oil and ceramic tableware are also notable.

## QA Qatar
- **LNG: #2 in the world (56% of exports).** The **North Field** is the world's largest natural gas field, shared with Iran's South Pars. **QatarEnergy** liquefies it at Ras Laffan and ships it to Asia and Europe.
- **Helium ("hydrogen, rare gases", #4 in the world)** is a by-product of LNG production; Qatar is the world's #2 helium producer. Sulphur (#2 in the world), fertilizer and polyethylene are also gas by-products.
- **Among the richest countries per capita**, with about 300,000 citizens and roughly 2.5 million migrant workers.

## RO Romania
- **Cars (Dacia in Mioveni, Ford in Craiova) and parts are 16%.** Dacia, owned by Renault since 1999, makes Europe's cheapest new cars (Sandero, Duster).
- **Wiring harnesses, control boards and seats** come from car suppliers like Aptiv, Continental and Yazaki, a big FDI base after EU accession in 2007.
- **The Danubian grain belt:** wheat, **sunflower seeds (#1 in the world)**, rapeseed and corn. Since 2022, Romania's **Constanța** port has also been the main route for Ukrainian grain exports.

## RU Russia
- **Energy is about 60%:** crude (#3 in the world), refined products, gas and coal (#3). It's still huge despite sanctions, but the buyers have shifted from Europe to **China, India and Turkey**, with discounted oil carried on a "shadow fleet" of tankers.
- **Wheat: #1 in the world (17% of the market)**. Russia went from importing grain in Soviet times to being the top exporter, through Black Sea ports (Novorossiysk).
- **Metals and chemicals:** aluminium (#2 in the world, **Rusal**), platinum and palladium (**Nornickel**, Norilsk), steel slabs (#1), fertilizer (#1-#2) and potash (#2, Uralkali). It's also #1 in the world for **nuclear reactors** (Rosatom builds plants abroad: Turkey, Egypt, Bangladesh).
- **Why so few manufactured goods?** A resource-rent economy, Soviet-era industry that didn't modernize, and the "Dutch disease" effect of a strong ruble during oil booms.

## RW Rwanda
- **Gold (64%) is widely believed to be largely smuggled from eastern DR Congo**, along with coltan ("niobium/tantalum ore", #7 in the world) and tin. UN experts accuse Rwanda of backing the **M23** rebels, who control Congolese mining areas.
- **Coffee and tea** are the genuinely Rwandan exports, high-grown and specialty quality.
- **Rwanda's development story** (post-1994 genocide) emphasizes services: tourism (mountain gorillas), conferences and logistics.

## BL Saint Barthélemy
- **A $30M total of refined copper, jewelry and fruit juice**, all re-exports or noise. St. Barts is a luxury tourism island (French, duty-free) for yachts and celebrities.

## SH Saint Helena
- **A $40M total, mostly fish and lobster** from its EEZ. Saint Helena is one of the most remote inhabited islands on Earth, and Napoleon's place of exile and death (1815-21).
- **Its airport only opened in 2016** ("the world's most useless airport" at first, because of wind shear). Most of the economy is UK-funded. Its famous coffee (Green Tipped Bourbon) is tiny.

## KN Saint Kitts and Nevis
- **Yachts (47%) are registry and charter noise.** Tiny electronics plants (measuring instruments, transformers) are a remnant of 1980s offshore assembly.
- **The real earners: citizenship by investment** (St. Kitts pioneered selling passports in 1984) and tourism. Sugar, the colonial economy, ended in 2005.

## LC Saint Lucia
- **Beer (19%): Piton beer** from the Windward & Leeward Brewery (Heineken-linked), sold around the Caribbean.
- **Refined petroleum** comes from the Buckeye oil transshipment terminal. The banana industry (Windward Islands) collapsed after EU preferences ended. Tourism dominates.

## MF Saint Maarten
- **A $20M total of scrap, jewelry and re-exports.** Sint Maarten is the Dutch half of an island shared with France, known for duty-free shopping and its airport, where planes land just above Maho Beach.

## PM Saint Pierre and Miquelon
- **Processed seafood (crab and molluscs, 78%) in a $1M total.** These are the last French territories in North America, a remnant of New France off Newfoundland. The cod fishery that sustained it ended with the 1992 Grand Banks moratorium.
- **A Prohibition-era boom:** it was a major rum-running base in the 1920s, smuggling liquor into the US.

## VC Saint Vincent and the Grenadines
- **Flour (17%) and animal feed come from the East Caribbean Flour Mills**, which supply neighbouring islands. The yachts are charter fleet movements in the Grenadines.
- **Arrowroot** was once a major export (St. Vincent was the world's largest producer). Bananas declined, and the 2021 La Soufrière eruption hit farming.

## WS Samoa
- **Wiring harnesses and electronics come from the old Yazaki plant**, which closed in 2017. What's left is mostly re-export noise.
- **Coconut products and fish** (nonu juice, coconut cream) are the real exports. Remittances from Samoans in New Zealand, Australia and the US are about 20% of GDP.

## SM San Marino
- **Bottling machines (23%) and food preparations** come from small industrial firms, integrated with Italy's Emilia-Romagna machinery district next door.
- **The world's oldest surviving republic (founded 301 AD, by tradition).** Its economy is banking, tourism and stamps and coins for collectors.

## SA Saudi Arabia
- **Crude oil: #1 in the world (63% of exports, 16% of the global market).** **Saudi Aramco** is the world's most profitable company. **Ghawar** is the largest conventional oil field ever found. The Saudis act as OPEC's "swing producer".
- **Petrochemicals are the main diversification:** **SABIC** makes polyethylene (#2 in the world), polypropylene (#1) and ethylene glycol (acyclic alcohol derivatives, #1). The industrial cities of **Jubail and Yanbu**, built from scratch in the 1970s, turn cheap associated gas into plastics.
- **Vision 2030** aims to diversify into mining (Ma'aden phosphates and aluminium), tourism and NEOM, but oil still dominates.

## SN Senegal
- **Refined petroleum (19%) is re-exports** from the Dakar refinery and port to landlocked Mali. Since 2024 it also includes new offshore oil from **Sangomar** (Woodside).
- **Gold (Sabodala, Endeavour) and phosphoric acid (#4 in the world)** come from the **ICS** phosphate-and-acid plant (Indorama).
- **Fish and peanuts:** pirogue fisheries, which are hurt by foreign trawlers (driving migration to the Canaries), and groundnuts, the colonial "peanut basin" crop. Bouillon cubes ("soups and broths", #8 in the world) come from Nestlé's Maggi factory.

## RS Serbia
- **Car parts and wiring:** wiring harnesses (Yazaki, Leoni), tires (**Linglong**, Chinese, at Zrenjanin; Michelin at Pirot), motors and seats. Fiat (Stellantis) makes cars at Kragujevac.
- **Copper (Zijin's Bor and Čukaru Peki mines)** and steel (HBIS Smederevo) show China's large investments in Serbia.
- **Frozen raspberries ("frozen fruits", #4 in the world)**: Serbia is one of the world's top raspberry producers, from the Arilje region. Electricity is also exported.

## SC Seychelles
- **Tuna is 80%, both frozen and canned.** The **Indian Ocean Tuna (IOT)** cannery in Victoria (Thai Union) is one of the world's largest. Seychelles is a hub for the European purse-seine fleet.
- **Tourism and the "blue economy" are the real story.** In 2018 Seychelles issued the world's first sovereign "blue bond" to fund ocean conservation.

## SL Sierra Leone
- **Iron ore is 57%**, from **Tonkolili** and Marampa. These mines have had a stop-start history after the Ebola epidemic (2014) and the African Minerals collapse.
- **Rutile (titanium ore, #5 in the world)**: Sierra Rutile is one of the world's largest natural rutile deposits.
- **Diamonds (8.7%)** were the "blood diamonds" that fuelled the 1991-2002 civil war (the film *Blood Diamond*), and they led to the creation of the Kimberley Process. Cocoa and bauxite are smaller.

## SG Singapore
- **Refined petroleum (18%, #4 in the world)** comes from **Jurong Island**'s refineries (ExxonMobil, Shell), plus ship bunkering. Singapore is the world's #1 bunkering port.
- **Integrated circuits (17%, #5 in the world)** come from Micron's memory fabs, GlobalFoundries, and chip testing. **Lithography and chip tools** ("machinery having individual functions") are also shipped from regional hubs here.
- **"Tanks and armored vehicles" at #1 in the world (62% of the market) is almost certainly a classification quirk.** It likely reflects ST Engineering defence exports plus re-exports and HS code misuse. Treat it as a curiosity.
- **Pharma and biotech** (Biopolis: Pfizer, GSK and Sanofi plants) come from deliberate government industrial policy. Singapore has no natural resources, so everything is location, logistics and policy.

## SK Slovakia
- **Cars are 31%.** Slovakia makes **the most cars per person in the world**: VW Bratislava (Touareg, Audi Q7, Porsche Cayenne bodies), Kia Žilina, Stellantis Trnava and **Jaguar Land Rover Nitra** (Defender), with Volvo coming.
- **Why?** Low wages, a central location, EU membership, the euro since 2009, and generous investment incentives. It's also a risk: the economy is very exposed to the auto cycle and EV transition.
- **Samsung TVs ("video displays", #5 in the world)** come from Galanta and Voderady. Railway freight cars (#1 in the world) are made by Tatravagónka.

## SI Slovenia
- **Packaged medicine is 30%!** **Krka** (Novo Mesto) and **Novartis/Sandoz's Lek** in Ljubljana are major generic drugmakers. Lek was one of the first European makers of penicillin outside the West (1950s).
- **Also:** cars (Renault Revoz in Novo Mesto makes Clios and Twingos), Gorenje appliances (heaters), electricity from the Krško nuclear plant (shared with Croatia), Goodyear tires and Akrapovič exhausts.
- **The richest ex-Yugoslav state**, with a strong export-oriented manufacturing base.

## SB Solomon Islands
- **Raw logs make up 49%**, mostly to China. They're logged at unsustainable rates, often by Malaysian firms, and the forests are expected to be exhausted within years.
- **Canned and frozen tuna** come from SolTuna in Noro, plus gold (Gold Ridge mine, reopening) and palm oil. The Solomons switched recognition from Taiwan to China in 2019, and a China security pact followed in 2022.

## SO Somalia
- **Live sheep and goats: 36% (#2 in the world).** They're shipped from **Berbera** (Somaliland) and Bosaso to Saudi Arabia, especially for the **Hajj sacrifices** (Eid al-Adha). Camels ("other animals") go the same way.
- **Gold (30%) is a re-export** via Dubai networks. Frankincense and myrrh ("insect resins"; Somalia is a top source of frankincense) are ancient Puntland trade goods.
- **Remittances (about $1.5B+) and aid** fund much of the economy. "Documents of title" is likely printed currency.

## ZA South Africa
- **The Bushveld Complex: #1 in the world for platinum (34% of the global market), chromium ore (83%) and manganese (43%).** South Africa holds about 80% of the world's platinum reserves, mined by Implats, Sibanye-Stillwater and Anglo American Platinum (now Valterra). Platinum is used mostly in catalytic converters.
- **Gold (17%)** comes from the **Witwatersrand**, the world's richest goldfield ever, which built Johannesburg after 1886. Plus coal (Richards Bay), iron ore (Kumba) and diamonds (De Beers).
- **Cars (6%) and pickups**: Mercedes East London, BMW Rosslyn, Toyota Durban and VW Kariega. These were built up to serve Africa and the EU with tariff incentives under the Automotive Production and Development Programme.
- **Citrus (#2 in the world) and dissolving pulp (#2; Sappi Saiccor)** are also notable. Persistent power cuts ("load-shedding" by Eskom) have held back industry.

## KR South Korea
- **Integrated circuits are 18.5% (#3 in the world)**: memory chips from **Samsung and SK Hynix** (DRAM and NAND; SK Hynix makes the HBM memory used in AI GPUs).
- **Why chips and not just cars?** In the 1980s, Samsung (under Lee Byung-chul) bet heavily on DRAM, investing counter-cyclically during downturns. State-backed credit (the chaebol model) let firms out-invest Japanese rivals, whose market share had been capped by US trade pressure.
- **Cars (9.7%; Hyundai and Kia)**, ships (#2 in the world; **HD Hyundai, Samsung Heavy and Hanwha Ocean** build most of the world's LNG carriers), refined petroleum (Korea imports crude and exports fuel) and batteries (LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, SK On).
- **Heavy-industry push:** Park Chung-hee's 1970s "Heavy and Chemical Industry Drive" created POSCO steel (#1 in the world for cold-rolled iron) and petrochemicals (#1 for cyclic hydrocarbons and styrene).

## SS South Sudan
- **Crude oil is 81%**, piped north through **Sudan** to Port Sudan. That's an awkward dependency on the country South Sudan seceded from in 2011. The pipeline has been disrupted by Sudan's civil war since 2023.
- **Total exports of $670M are low** for a country with significant oil. Production collapsed during the 2013-18 civil war, and corruption absorbs the revenue. Gum arabic, gold and livestock are informal.

## ES Spain
- **Cars are 10.7%** (#2 car producer in Europe after Germany): **SEAT** (VW) in Martorell, plus Stellantis, Renault and Ford plants. Spain has no big domestic brand besides SEAT/Cupra.
- **Europe's market garden:** #1 in the world for **pork** (18% of the market; pigs from Catalonia and Aragon, much of it for China), **olive oil** (40% of the market, mostly Andalusia's Jaén province), citrus (Valencia), lettuce, cucumbers and melons (from the greenhouses of **Almería**, a "sea of plastic" visible from space).
- **Pharma (Grifols plasma products) and Zara (Inditex)**: Inditex's clothing is made mainly in Asia, Morocco and Turkey, so it shows up only partly here.

## LK Sri Lanka
- **Garments are 44%,** especially **women's underwear and lingerie (#3 in the world)**. **MAS Holdings** and Brandix make Victoria's Secret, Marks & Spencer and Nike goods in "ethical" factories.
- **Ceylon tea (#3 in the world, 16% of the market)** grows in the hill country (Nuwara Eliya), introduced by the British after coffee rust wiped out coffee in the 1870s.
- **Niches:** cinnamon (#2 in the world; true "Ceylon" cinnamon), coir ("coconut fibers", #2), rubber gloves and solid tires (Camso/Michelin), and sapphires ("precious stones", from Ratnapura).
- **Sri Lanka defaulted on its debt in 2022** after a currency crisis, so exports are a lifeline.

## SD Sudan
- **Gold (21%) and oil (20%)**, with gold largely controlled by the RSF paramilitary (Hemedti) and the army, and much of it flown to the UAE. The **2023- civil war** between them has wrecked the economy, so these 2023 figures are already falling.
- **#1 in the world for sesame** ("other oily seeds") and **live sheep and goats**: Sudanese Hadhari sheep are shipped to Saudi Arabia.
- **Gum arabic (#3 in the world for "insect resins/gums")**: Sudan's acacia belt supplies about 70% of the world's gum arabic, used in Coca-Cola and candy. The US exempted it from sanctions for that reason. Also peanuts and groundnut meal (#1 in the world).

## SR Suriname
- **Gold is 77%**: **Rosebel** (Zijin) and **Merian** (Newmont), plus artisanal mining, often by Brazilian garimpeiros in the interior.
- **Bauxite and alumina** (Alcoa's Suralco) dominated for a century but ended in 2017. **Oil is coming:** TotalEnergies' offshore **GranMorgu** project, next door to Guyana's boom, is due around 2028.
- **The smallest country in South America**, a former Dutch colony (traded for New Amsterdam/New York in 1667).

## SE Sweden
- **Vehicles: Volvo Cars** (Gothenburg, Chinese-owned by Geely), **Volvo trucks and Scania** (combustion engines, chassis, truck cabs). Sweden is a big heavy-truck producer.
- **Forest industry:** sawn wood (#3 in the world), kraft paper (#2) and coated paper (SCA, Stora Enso, Holmen).
- **Engineering niches:** iron powder (#1 in the world, **Höganäs**), ion-exchangers, ball bearings (**SKF**), nuclear reactor parts (#2, Westinghouse Västerås) and Ericsson telecoms. Pharma (AstraZeneca's Södertälje plant) is significant.
- **Why so many global firms for 10 million people?** Iron ore, timber and hydropower supported early industrialization, along with the Wallenberg family's long-term ownership (Investor AB).

## CH Switzerland
- **Gold is 27% (#1 in the world, 19% of the market).** Switzerland **refines about two-thirds of the world's gold** in Ticino refineries (Valcambi, PAMP, Argor-Heraeus), turning doré from mines and London bars into kilobars for Asia.
- **Pharma (~25%): Roche and Novartis** in Basel. Basel's dye industry (Ciba, Geigy, Sandoz) followed the same dye-to-drug path as Germany's, helped by lax 19th-century Swiss patent laws that allowed copying.
- **Watches: #1 in the world (65-69% of the market).** Rolex, Swatch Group (Omega) and Richemont are based in the Jura arc. **Huguenot refugees** brought watchmaking to Geneva in the 1500s.
- **Medtech** includes Straumann dental implants and Synthes orthopedics (#2 in the world). Electricity (#3) comes from pumped-storage hydro trading in the Alps.

## SY Syria
- **Olive oil is 24%.** Syria is one of olive cultivation's ancestral homelands, with groves around Idlib and Aleppo. Phosphates come from Palmyra-area mines.
- **Cumin and anise ("spice seeds", #5 in the world)** and **Aleppo pepper and soap** reflect an ancient trade economy.
- **Exports are only $1.3B, a fraction of pre-war levels.** Before 2011, oil was about a quarter of exports. The war, sanctions and the **captagon** drug trade (hundreds of millions of dollars, invisible here) reshaped the economy. The Assad regime fell in December 2024.

## ST São Tomé and Príncipe
- **A $60M total.** "Crude petroleum" likely reflects fuel and oil-service movements, since there's no domestic production yet. Offshore exploration (Shell, Galp) continues in the Joint Development Zone with Nigeria.
- **Cocoa (17%)** is the historic crop. Around 1910 São Tomé was briefly **the world's #1 cocoa producer**, using forced labour from Angola, which caused a British chocolate boycott (Cadbury, 1909). Today it sells small-batch organic cocoa.

## TW Taiwan
- **Integrated circuits are 43%: #1 in the world, a quarter of the global market.** **TSMC** makes about 90% of the world's most advanced chips (for Apple, Nvidia and AMD) in Hsinchu, Taichung and Tainan.
- **Why Taiwan?** In 1987 the government-backed ITRI spun out TSMC under **Morris Chang** with a novel "pure-play foundry" model: manufacture chips for anyone, design none. That let fabless firms like Nvidia exist. Decades of compounding process know-how followed ("the silicon shield").
- **The ICT supply chain:** servers and computers (Foxconn, Quanta and Wistron make AI servers), PCBs (#2 in the world), copper foil (#1), lenses (#3; **Largan** makes iPhone camera lenses) and screws/fasteners (#3).
- **Bicycles (#2 in the world): Giant and Merida**, a cluster around Taichung, plus scooter parts.

## TJ Tajikistan
- **Gold (24%), cotton (10%), aluminium and antimony.** Antimony is **#1 in the world (32% of the market)**, from the Anzob/Konchoch mines (TALCO Gold, Chinese-partnered). Antimony is a critical mineral for munitions and flame retardants.
- **Aluminium comes from TALCO**, a Soviet-era smelter running on cheap hydropower from the Nurek dam, with the Rogun dam under construction.
- **Remittances are the giant: about 40-50% of GDP**, among the highest in the world, from migrant workers in Russia.

## TZ Tanzania
- **Gold is 42%.** **Geita** (AngloGold Ashanti) and Barrick's North Mara and Bulyanhulu, around Lake Victoria, make Tanzania one of Africa's top gold producers.
- **Tanzanite** (found only near Arusha) shows up under precious stones. Cashews, sesame, coffee (Kilimanjaro), tobacco and pulses are the farm side.
- **Transit hub:** refined petroleum and copper (from Zambia and DRC) pass through **Dar es Salaam**, and the TAZARA railway links it to Zambia. Tourism (Serengeti, Zanzibar) is large too.

## TH Thailand
- **"The Detroit of Asia": pickup trucks (#4 in the world for delivery trucks).** Toyota Hilux, Isuzu D-Max and Ford Ranger are made around the Eastern Seaboard. Thai tax policy has favored one-ton pickups since the 1980s.
- **Hard drives ("office machine parts", #3 in the world)**: Western Digital and Seagate assemble most of the world's hard disk drives in Thailand. The 2011 floods caused a global HDD shortage. Air conditioners (#2 in the world; Daikin, Mitsubishi) and electronics also feature.
- **Farm #1s in the world:** rubber (28% of the market), cassava and starch, and durian ("other fruits", mostly for China), plus rice, sugar and canned tuna (Thai Union, the world's largest tuna canner).
- **Why Japan's manufacturing base?** After the 1985 Plaza Accord strengthened the yen, Japanese firms moved factories to Thailand, and supplier clusters followed.

## TL Timor-Leste
- **Oil and gas (74%)** come from the depleting **Bayu-Undan** field, shared with Australia (which ceased production in 2025). Oil money funds a sovereign Petroleum Fund that's being drawn down fast.
- **Coffee (12%)** is organic Arabica from the highlands around Ermera. Starbucks buys it. The **Greater Sunrise** field is the future hope, disputed for years with Australia (including a spying scandal).

## TG Togo
- **Gold (37%)** is mostly re-exported from Burkina and the Sahel, not mined in Togo. Refined petroleum (27%) is also re-exports via Lomé, a deep-water port and regional transit hub.
- **Phosphates (#5 in the world)** come from the coastal Hahotoé mines, Togo's historic big export. Soybeans, cashews and cotton are smallholder crops.

## TK Tokelau
- **"Cars" at 89% of a $170M total is a data artifact.** Tokelau has about 1,500 people on three atolls, no roads to speak of, and no car production. It's probably a misattributed shipment.
- **Its real incomes are New Zealand aid, fishing licenses and the .tk domain** (once the world's most-registered free domain).

## TO Tonga
- **A $20M total of noise.** "Documents of title" is likely printed stamps and currency, plus fuel and re-exports.
- **The real exports are squash (pumpkin) to Japan**, kava, vanilla and fish. **Remittances (about 40% of GDP)** are among the world's highest share. Tonga was hit by the Hunga Tonga volcano eruption and tsunami in 2022.

## TT Trinidad and Tobago
- **A gas-based petrochemical economy:** LNG (30%; **Atlantic LNG** at Point Fortin), **ammonia (#1 in the world, 20% of the market)** and **methanol** (#5 in the world for acyclic alcohols), all from the **Point Lisas** industrial estate, built in the 1970s-80s to monetize offshore gas.
- **Iron reductions (DRI, #3 in the world)** are steel feedstock (Nucor, ArcelorMittal), made with cheap gas. Trinidad is a textbook case of using gas to build industry rather than only exporting it.
- **Pitch Lake**, the world's largest natural asphalt lake, also produces export asphalt. Angostura bitters is a famous Trinidadian product.

## TN Tunisia
- **Wiring harnesses (14%) and car electronics** come from Yazaki, Dräxlmaier and Leoni plants, built for European carmakers since the 1990s.
- **Clothing (18%)** includes jeans and workwear for European brands, a near-shoring base for France and Italy.
- **Olive oil (#5 in the world; Tunisia is often #2 by volume)** comes from Sfax's vast groves, much of it sold in bulk to Italy and Spain. Phosphates (Gafsa) were historically huge, but production has been disrupted since 2011.

## TR Turkey
- **A broad, mid-tech manufacturing economy**, a "near-shore" supplier to Europe through the 1995 EU customs union. Cars (Ford Otosan, Toyota, Renault Oyak), buses (#2 in the world; Mercedes Hoşdere, Otokar), white goods (**Arçelik/Beko**) and steel.
- **Textiles (14%) and clothing:** #3 in the world for women's suits and T-shirts, plus denim. Turkey is Zara's and H&M's fast-fashion supplier because delivery takes days rather than weeks from Asia.
- **#1s in the world:** borates (55% of the market; state-owned Eti Maden holds about 70% of world reserves), hand-woven rugs, marble (Afyon, Muğla) and wheat flour (made from imported Russian wheat, re-exported to the Middle East and Africa).
- **Gold and jewelry (Istanbul's Grand Bazaar)** reflect Turks' habit of saving in gold during high inflation.

## TM Turkmenistan
- **Gas is 71%.** **Galkynysh** is among the world's largest gas fields. Most of it goes to **China** via the Central Asia-China pipeline (CNPC). Russia stopped buying in 2016.
- **One of the world's most closed regimes** ("the North Korea of Central Asia"), and gas export politics shape everything. Cotton, cotton yarn and fertilizer are smaller. The "Door to Hell" gas crater at Darvaza is its most famous landmark.

## TC Turks and Caicos Islands
- **A $20M total of noise:** plastics and chemicals re-exports, plus conch ("molluscs") and spiny lobster, the real fisheries.
- **Turks and Caicos has the world's only commercial conch farm** (now closed). Its real economy is luxury tourism (Grace Bay) and offshore finance.

## TV Tuvalu
- **Frozen tuna is 94%**, from foreign vessels licensed in its EEZ.
- **Tuvalu's famous "export" is the .tv domain**, licensed to Verisign/GoDaddy and booming thanks to Twitch. It brings in a meaningful share of government revenue. With about 11,000 people and a highest point of about 4.6m, Tuvalu is threatened by sea-level rise, and in 2023 it signed a migration treaty with Australia.

## UG Uganda
- **Gold (45%) is largely refined imports.** The **African Gold Refinery** in Entebbe processes gold of disputed origin, partly from DRC and South Sudan.
- **Coffee (14%)** is mostly Robusta, which is native to Uganda's forests. Uganda is Africa's #2 coffee exporter after Ethiopia.
- **Nile perch** ("dried fish") from Lake Victoria, vanilla (#2 in the world) and flowers. **Oil is coming:** the Lake Albert fields and the **EACOP** heated pipeline to Tanga (TotalEnergies, CNOOC) are due around 2026.

## UA Ukraine
- **"The breadbasket of Europe": corn (#4 in the world), sunflower oil (#1 in the world, 28% of the market)**, wheat, soy and rapeseed from the chernozem black-earth soils. Ukraine produces about a third of the world's sunflower oil.
- **War-shaped:** these 2023 numbers come from the **Black Sea grain corridor** era. Exports fell sharply from about $68B in 2021, and steel collapsed after **Mariupol's Azovstal** and Illich works were destroyed. Iron ore and pig iron (#3 in the world) remain from Kryvyi Rih.
- **Wiring harnesses** from western Ukraine (Leoni, Fujikura) supply German carmakers. Ukraine also made the world's largest plane, the An-225 "Mriya", which was destroyed in 2022.

## AE United Arab Emirates
- **Oil (29% crude and 16% refined)** comes from Abu Dhabi (**ADNOC**) and Ruwais, Dubai's own oil having largely run out.
- **Dubai is a global re-export hub:** gold (#2 in the world), diamonds (#2), jewelry and phones flow through **Jebel Ali** port and the gold souk. Since 2022 the UAE has been a key conduit for Russian and African gold.
- **Aluminium (#3 in the world): EGA** (Emirates Global Aluminium), one of the world's largest smelters, uses cheap gas. Sulphur (#1 in the world, from Shah sour gas) and cigarettes (#2 in the world, re-exports) are also notable.
- **Why diversified?** Dubai's oil was always small, so Sheikh Rashid built ports (1970s), a free zone and an airline (**Emirates**) to become a trading hub.

## GB United Kingdom
- **Gas turbines at #2 in the world are Rolls-Royce jet engines** (Derby), and aircraft parts (#3) are Airbus wings made at **Broughton** in Wales. These are the remains of a once-world-leading aircraft industry.
- **Cars (8.6%)**: Jaguar Land Rover (Indian-owned), **Nissan Sunderland**, Mini (BMW, Oxford) and Toyota. Almost all of them are foreign-owned after the 1970s decline of British Leyland.
- **Scotch whisky: #1 in the world for "hard liquor" (24% of the market)**, from Diageo and Pernod's distilleries.
- **London's markets:** gold (#4 in the world, LBMA vaults), paintings (#2, Christie's and Sotheby's) and North Sea oil and gas. Still, the UK is mainly a **services exporter** (finance, consulting, education), and its goods trade runs a large deficit.

## US United States
- **#1 in the world for energy exports:** LNG (since 2022, through Gulf Coast terminals like Sabine Pass), refined fuels and crude (#2). This is the **shale revolution**: fracking in Texas's Permian basin turned the US from an importer into the world's largest oil and gas producer.
- **High-tech #1s:** gas turbines (33% of the market; **GE Aerospace** and Pratt & Whitney jet engines), aircraft (28%; **Boeing**), aircraft parts and vaccines (Pfizer, Moderna).
- **Agriculture:** soybeans (#2 in the world), corn, cotton (#1, 35% of the market), and almonds and pistachios ("other nuts", #1, 45% of the market; California's Central Valley). Corn ethanol by-products ("starch residue", 56% of the market) are a big feed export.
- **Exports are only about 7-8% of GDP** because the US has a huge domestic market. That's why its treemap is so diversified, and why it runs persistent trade deficits.

## UY Uruguay
- **Wood pulp is 20%**, from Finnish **UPM** (Fray Bentos and the giant UPM 2 at Paso de los Toros) and Chilean-Swedish **Montes del Plata**. Eucalyptus grows about 10 times faster here than in Scandinavia. The pulp mills sparked a famous dispute with Argentina at the International Court of Justice.
- **Beef (18%)**: Uruguay has more cattle than people (about 12 million vs 3.5 million) and pioneered **100% cattle traceability** (since 2006), which lets it command premium prices.
- **Also:** rice, milk powder, malt (to Brazil's breweries) and Coca-Cola concentrate ("scented mixtures"). Uruguay also runs on about 95% renewable electricity.

## UZ Uzbekistan
- **Gold is 46%.** **Muruntau** in the Kyzylkum Desert is one of the world's largest open-pit gold mines, run by the state's Navoi Mining.
- **From raw cotton to cotton yarn (#3 in the world):** after ending forced child labour in cotton picking (around 2021), Uzbekistan banned raw cotton exports to force domestic spinning and garment-making.
- **Also:** copper (Almalyk), uranium, molybdenum and wheat flour for Afghanistan and Tajikistan. Russian-market re-exports are also present.

## VU Vanuatu
- **Frozen tuna (60%)** is caught in its EEZ under foreign licensing deals.
- **Kava ("perfume plants", 10%)** is a sedative root drink with a growing export market to Fiji, the US and the Pacific diaspora, alongside copra and cocoa.
- **Vanuatu sells "citizenship by investment"** (passports) and runs a ship registry (the ships slice).

## VE Venezuela
- **Only $7.7B: a petrostate in collapse.** Venezuela has the **world's largest proven oil reserves** (Orinoco heavy-oil belt), but exports fell from about $90B (2012) after Chávez/Maduro-era mismanagement of **PDVSA** and US sanctions (2017-19).
- **Crude (53%), petroleum coke and methanol.** Much of it goes to China, some via **Chevron**'s special US licence. Iron and aluminium (CVG Guayana) are remnants of state-led industrialization.
- **Scrap metal (5.5%)** is a sign of decline: dismantled infrastructure and factories are being sold abroad.

## VN Vietnam
- **Phones are 18% (#2 in the world): Samsung** makes about half its smartphones at Bac Ninh and Thai Nguyen, and Samsung alone accounts for roughly a fifth of Vietnam's exports.
- **"China+1":** electronics, computers (Foxconn, Luxshare for Apple), furniture (#2 in the world) and shoes (#2 in the world; **Nike and Adidas** make about half their shoes here). Firms diversified out of China after US tariffs from 2018.
- **Food #1s:** cashews (30% of the market; Vietnam imports raw nuts from Africa and shells them), plus pepper (#2), rice, coffee (#2 producer of Robusta) and pangasius catfish.
- **Why Vietnam?** Đổi Mới reforms (1986), cheap and disciplined labour, many trade deals (CPTPP, EU-Vietnam FTA), and a border with China's supply chains.

## WF Wallis and Futuna
- **Under $1M of seats, electronics and car parts**: re-export noise. This is a French Polynesian territory of about 11,000 people, ruled partly by three traditional kings, with a subsistence economy and French subsidies.

## YE Yemen
- **Only $1.1B: a war economy.** Oil exports stopped in 2022 after Houthi drone attacks on export terminals. Gold is re-exported, and scrap metal is war salvage.
- **Fish and cuttlefish** come from the Gulf of Aden. Yemen is famous for **Mocha coffee**: the port of Mocha was the world's coffee export hub in the 1600s. Today Yemen is a leading consumer of **qat**, a stimulant leaf grown domestically that uses scarce water.
- **The Houthi Red Sea shipping attacks (2023-24)** disrupted global trade through Bab-el-Mandeb.

## ZM Zambia
- **Copper is 63%**, with raw (blister) copper at **#1 in the world (43% of the market)**. The **Copperbelt** (Kitwe, Chingola, Kansanshi, Lumwana) has been mined since the 1920s. Konkola was owned by Vedanta, Mopani by the state, and Chinese and Canadian firms (First Quantum, Barrick) hold others.
- **"Dutch disease" textbook case:** copper made Zambia one of Africa's richest countries at independence (1964), but the price crash of the 1970s led to decades of decline. It defaulted on its debt in 2020.
- **Emeralds ("precious stones", #8 in the world; Gemfields' Kagem mine, the world's largest emerald mine)**, gold, tobacco and cobalt are smaller.

## ZW Zimbabwe
- **Gold (40%)** is largely from small-scale miners selling to Fidelity Gold Refinery. Some is smuggled.
- **Tobacco (#2 in the world)**: Virginia flue-cured leaf, mostly sold to **China**. After the 2000s land reform, smallholders took over production from white commercial farmers, and output recovered.
- **"Other mineral" (#2 in the world) is lithium!** Chinese firms (Huayou, Sinomine) bought the **Arcadia and Bikita** mines and ship spodumene concentrate to China. Zimbabwe then banned raw ore exports to push for local processing.
- **The Great Dyke** yields platinum and nickel (Zimplats' nickel matte, #4 in the world) and chromium. Marange diamonds are a further mining slice.
